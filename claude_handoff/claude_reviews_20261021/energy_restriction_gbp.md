## 1. Hypotheses (this reviewer's, stated before evidence)

- E1 (arithmetic is exact): both GBP families compute the exact mean and exact marginal variance of their *declared* quadratic energy on a forest, with source-once cavities, replacement messages and no posterior re-injection. (Expected true; checked.)
- E2 (semantic error, not solver error): the declared energies treat each agent's full-map code μ_i as a noisy measurement of one complete-map variable with precision q_i>0 in every coordinate. For complementary partial observations this is wrong by construction, and no setting of a diagonal q, of diagonal/permutation restriction gains, or of a shared orthogonal basis can repair it when the code is dense (coordinates not spatially owned). Prediction: own-region quality must fall already at R1 with ≤3 sources, monotonically in |S| and in content diversity; learned restrictions will either dilute (equality) or close channels (unit-noise), never compose.
- E3 (what fixes it is upstream of GBP): if source factors had coordinate ownership (q_ik≈0 whenever agent i observed nothing of the region owned by k) and consistency (observers of the same owned region emit the same value), then the *existing* diagonal GBP (either family) composes partial maps exactly; the restriction family only has to be diagonal-closed (scaled partial permutations). Hence a GBP/energy change alone cannot move wall F1; K0 is GBP-independent exactly.

### 2(b) Which restriction families keep edge messages exactly diagonal

With a diagonal cavity Q and edge factor ½‖A x_i − B x_j‖² (unit noise), the precision delivered to x_j is P = Bᵀ(A Q⁻¹ Aᵀ + I)⁻¹B. (A Q⁻¹ Aᵀ)_{mn} = Σ_k A_mk A_nk / q_k vanishes for all q iff no column of A has two nonzeros; (Bᵀ D B)_{kl} = Σ_m B_mk B_ml d_m vanishes for all d>0 iff no row of B has two nonzeros. Both directions ⇒ A and B are scaled partial permutations (at most one nonzero per row and per column), up to a common left orthogonal factor on the edge stalk that cancels (‖U(Dx_i − DPx_j)‖ = ‖Dx_i − DPx_j‖, an unobservable gauge). Numerically (T8, d=6, random Q): positive diagonal (current v324 family), signed scaled permutation, partial selection (3 of 6): off-diagonal 0.0 in both directions; dense generic: 0.51; orthogonal U at one end and I at the other: 0.12/0.14; row-sum [1 1 0 …] at one end: diagonal in one direction, off-diagonal 1.0 in the other. **So the README's and root's statement is correct: positive diagonal scaling, signed permutations and partial selection keep messages exactly diagonal; any genuinely mixing restriction (dense, or different orthogonal charts at the two ends) breaks closure and "dense then diagonalize" silently discards information.** (v319 is not a mixing edge: its edge stays the identity in y=Ux, which is gauge-equivalent to the identity in x; what v319 changes is the *source* family — precisions diagonal in one shared rotated basis, `mapping_restriction_v319.py:94-97` — and T4(ii) applies verbatim in that basis unless U happens to align every agent's unobserved subspace with coordinate axes, which is ownership again.) What is discarded (T8, Q=[[1,1],[1,1]]+1e-6, an agent that observed only x₁+x₂): diag(Q) [= diag(JᵀJ)] fabricates precision 1.0 along the unobserved (1,−1) and halves the observed (1,1) (2.0→1.0); the v300 moment-matched 1/Cov_kk drops the observed direction to 2e-6. Neither preserves the constraint structure; README §4.2's "diag(JᵀJ) can destroy nullspaces" is confirmed and the v300 alternative is no better.

Two semantics traps in family B worth recording (T3): a sender gain a→0 is **not** silence — it transmits precision Q/(Q+a²)→1 with information→0, i.e. a fake observation "z=0" that pulls the receiver toward 0 (node with μ=5 moved to 2.50 by a peer with a=1e-3, b=1). True silence on a coordinate needs the *receiver* gain b→0 (or a→∞). The `.1+softplus` floor therefore never reaches silence: at a=b=.1 a message still carries 0.0099 precision (≈1 % of own weight); at init (a=b=.793, q=1.0) a first-hop message carries 0.386 of the receiver's own weight; relayed through k empty nodes with a=b=1 the precision decays as 1/(k+2) (0.333, 0.250, 0.167, 0.125 for k=1,2,4,6; closed form matched). Unit-noise edges are therefore a *diffusion* channel that the model can only turn into equality by growing a=b (a=b=10 gives 0.99, a=b=230 gives 1.000 — T3); the v340 audit shows exactly this bimodal outcome: `restriction_near_floor_fraction` 0.871, `restriction_max` 181–280 (`audit.json: arms.control.activity.source_saturation`).

### 2(c) Semantics required for precision-weighted averaging to be correct fusion; dense-code counterexample; link to R8 room merging

Equality-family fusion is mean_k = Σ_i q_ik μ_ik / Σ_i q_ik, var_k = 1/Σ_i q_ik. For this to equal the correct fusion of two partial maps M₁∪M₂ one needs, coordinate by coordinate:
- O1 (ownership/locality): there is a public region S_k with μ_ik = g_k(M_i|S_k) — coordinate k's value depends only on the map inside S_k, not on which other regions were seen;
- O2 (abstention): q_ik = 0 (or the prior) whenever agent i observed nothing of S_k;
- O3 (consistency/additivity): agents that did observe S_k agree (noiseless) or g_k is an additive sufficient statistic (e.g. per-cell log-odds, where Σh/Σq *is* exact occupancy-grid fusion);
- O4 (readable support): the decoder must be able to read "no evidence" from var_k = 1/Σq (needs q_unobserved ≪ q_observed; with [1e-4,4] the log-variance range is 10.6 nats, enough in principle).
Under O1–O3 the average is exactly the union (T4(i): ownership code with q=1e-7 off-support fuses (1,·),(·,1) to (1,1) to 1e-7); the v324 unit-noise edge also recovers (0.9999, 0.9998) when the receiver abstains (T4(v)).

**Counterexample for dense codes (T4(ii)).** Two features (fA,fB)∈{0,1}², a 45° code z=R(fA,fB), agent 1 sees only A and must fill fB with its "unobserved" default u (0), agent 2 symmetric. The precision agent 1 would need in z-space is R·diag(1,0)·Rᵀ = ½[[1,1],[1,1]]: rank one and off-diagonal = diagonal, not representable by any diag(q). Grid search over all four diagonal precisions in {1e-6,…,10}: best achievable max-error is **exactly 0.500** (x̂=(0.5,0.5)): both walls land on the decision threshold. Closed form: z₁=(1,1)/√2, z₂=(1,−1)/√2, so coordinate 1 is agreed on and coordinate 2 is some convex combination t∈[0,1] of the two votes; x̂ = Rᵀ(mean_z) = (t, 1−t), hence max|x̂−(1,1)| ≥ ½ for *every* positive diagonal weighting, not just the grid. Any diagonal weight that lets agent 1 speak about fA also makes it vote u for fB. With N complementary sources and equal q, each feature becomes f/N + (N−1)u/N = 0.0625 for N=16 (T4(iii)): the fused code is the dataset centroid. With family-B unit-noise edges, a=b=1, the two-node fused x̂ is (0.667,0.333)/(0.333,0.667) (T4(iv)) — the receiver's own default outweighs the peer's content 2:1.

### 2(d) "Local code as the mean of a global variable" — is ½Σq(x−μ)² a valid observation model?

Only if x* − μ_i | obs_i is zero-mean with diagonal covariance 1/q_i, independent across agents. For a deterministic encoder of a partial map, x* − Enc(M_i) is a deterministic function of the unobserved part: not zero-mean, correlated across agents that miss the same region, and nonzero in every coordinate of a dense code. The energy is therefore a valid likelihood only for the redundant-observation regime (agents that saw the same content, codes agree) and is wrong for complementary observations, which is the regime that matters for a union. If one insists on a probabilistic reading (μ_i = E[x|obs_i], q_i = conditional precision), the correct fusion is a product of posteriors divided by the prior: P = Σq_i − (|S|−1)P₀, η = Σh_i; the current energies omit the prior division, so every "unobserved = prior" coordinate is counted |S| times — the mechanism behind "unknown pushes free/erases walls". Even that correction only works if q_ik reduces to P₀ coordinate-wise where nothing was seen, i.e. again O2. The v340 audit shows O2 is not learned: fixed-panel source q over 16 active sources min .40, median 1.38, max 3.54 (`configs/mapping_endpoint_v340_fp_energy.json: fixed_GPU_statistics.source_q`), per-scene minimum over 1184 TRAIN steps .20–.61 and `precision_floor_fraction` 0.0 (`audit.json: arms.control.activity.source_saturation`); README §4.2's "almost every latent direction has positive precision" is confirmed. The model's gradient found the only other way to stop dilution available to it — close 87 % of the channels — rather than abstain, which is what 2(c) predicts when no coordinate is cleanly unobserved.

What the observation factor *should* constrain: the information form (AᵀΛA, AᵀΛb) whose row space is the subspace of the global variable that the agent's pixels inform. For the actual pixel-BCE observation model through a decoder f, this is the Gauss–Newton J_iᵀW_iJ_i over *observed* pixels only (`mapping_observation_energy_v328.py:75-116` already measures directional GGN curvature). Directions that must not be constrained: the (near-)nullspace of J_i (decoded pixels outside the own support), and the own known/unknown outline as a *constraint on others* — "unknown" is neither free nor "no peer content here". A diagonal GBP can carry J_iᵀW_iJ_i without loss only when all agents' GGNs are simultaneously (near-)diagonal in the shared coordinates; for generic overlapping supports that happens iff the coordinates are owned by regions (commuting projections ⇔ common eigenbasis ⇔ direct-sum code over a spatial partition). This is the same ownership condition as O1–O2, now derived from the likelihood side. Note also that in both encoders q_i doubles as a content channel into the decoder (`v324.py:302, 324`; v284 log-var feature), so q cannot be calibrated and used as a gate at the same time.

## 3. Counter-evidence and caveats

- The overlap statistics do not cleanly separate "redundant vs complementary" from "simple vs complex layout" as the driver of the own-F1 drop (rooms6/N8 has low overlap and a small drop). D1 is designed to separate mean mixing from the variance feature and from decoder capacity; until it runs, "dilution by disagreeing codes" is a strongly consistent explanation, not a measured causal share.
- At 512 the anchor's own F1 *rises* with communication (rooms6/N16 .509→.554, rooms12/N16 .337→.384): where K0 is poor (dotted far-range coverage, F3), averaging with overlapping peers helps. Averaging is right for redundancy; the failure is specific to complementarity. A pure "stop averaging" rule would lose this.
- Family-B own-region protection in v340 comes from the source-anchored readout (`v329.ownership_blend`), not from GBP; its cost (channels closed, peer F1 low, FPR .17–.31) is a readout/representation outcome too. These are 1-epoch models (E17) and weak in absolute terms (K0 .38–.64 at 128), so their numbers bound mechanisms, not capacity.
- Positive precision everywhere is not automatically wrong (finite noise, priors): the error is treating a *reconstruction* code as a *likelihood* center. If an encoder emitted conditional means μ_i=E[x|obs_i] with calibrated q_i, prior-corrected PoE would be the right energy even for dense codes — but T4(ii) still holds for the uncorrected energy, and calibration of a dense conditional-mean encoder is not something the pixel-BCE objective asks for.
- Diagonal closure is sufficient, not necessary, for *usefulness*: a dense edge with an honest dense (2080-number) message or a low-rank message could compose dense codes, at a wire cost the 64+64 protocol does not allow (README §4.2 last paragraph). I did not evaluate low-rank alternatives.
- The 2-D counterexample uses a fixed "unobserved default" u; a learned encoder could emit a per-coordinate u that minimizes expected damage (e.g. the population mean), which is what the centroid collapse in F5 looks like, not a fix.
- Source-once does not make overlapping observations independent (README §4.2): two agents seeing the same wall add their precisions (var 1.35→0.06 at |S|=8 in T6). This over-confidence is harmless to the mean when codes agree but feeds the decoder's variance input; its effect size is unmeasured (D1's `redundant` arm bounds it).

## 5. Verdict on H1/H2/H3

- H1 (distance-field head): outside this role; from the energy side only: whatever raises K0 must act on encoder/decoder/loss because K0 is GBP-independent (T2). Plausible for recall at 256/512; it does not address the R1 own-loss mechanism. Confidence that it changes K0 F1 materially: medium; that it changes fusion semantics: low.
- H2 (fusion semantics): the negative half — diagonal Gaussian averaging of dense codes cannot compose complementary partial maps — is proven (T4(ii)) and matches every empirical signature available (v310 R1 drop at 2.8 sources, monotone in |S| and rooms; v319 shared-U null result; v340 channel closing; root's 20 pp oracle gap at 128): high confidence (≈0.9). The positive half — ownership + abstention makes the existing diagonal GBP exact — is proven in the Gaussian toy (T4(i),(v)) and diagonal-closure of selection/permutation maps is proven (T8): high confidence as mathematics; whether 64 owned variables can carry pixel-exact walls is unproven: medium-low (≈0.4) without a multi-bit-per-block design. Correction to the root's phrasing: selection/permutation restrictions are necessary *not to break* composition but do not create it; composition is a property of the source factors (O1–O3).
- H3 (ceiling at 512/20 rooms): at 128 the bit budget is not binding (root: hard8 == continuous; oracle R8 .78 with the frozen decoder) — high confidence; at 512 with 1-px lines a 64×8-bit cap is plausible but no evidence here separates it from decoder limits: medium (≈0.5). The energy review adds that the 64-*variable* count, not bits, becomes the binding constraint once ownership is imposed.
