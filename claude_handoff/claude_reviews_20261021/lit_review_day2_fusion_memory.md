## 1. What was built (as read in the code, for precision)

`rollout_max` (`train_v347_maxsel.py` L261-298): each source factor (p_own = mask·q + α, η_own = mask·q·μ, α = 1e-4/(64N)) is quantised once with the sealed profile-4 wire (`forest.quantize`, L274); a directed message i→j carries, per coordinate, the arg-max-precision factor among {wire(i)} ∪ {messages into i except from j} (L291-293); a node's belief is the arg-max among {own unquantised factor} ∪ {incoming} (L278-280); ties go to the first candidate (own first; L257-258). Features = (hard8(η/P), log 1/P). On a forest with R=8 synchronous rounds; `allow_cycles` only in the tests.

`eval_dynamic_v349.py`: per step t each node joins its own quantised factor (version t) into a persistent per-coordinate memory (p, η, version-of-winner) with `join` = arg-max of p + 1e-9·version (L57-62, L108), floods R rounds on the current radio forest with cavity exclusion (L111-131), receivers join everything (L133-137); a version vector V[i][j] (latest version of source j known to i) is carried in every packet and joined by max (L117-121, L136-137) and is used only for the target definition (L142-146) and the tie-break. Bytes: 8 + 8 + 2·changed + N if any code changed, else 8 + N if only the version vector changed, else 0 (L129); the stateless-max control is billed 8 + 8 + 2·changed (L165); the sealed sum control is billed full 172 B per directed packet per round (L174).

## 2. Q1. Where max-precision selection sits in the literature

### 2.1 Gaussian / information fusion and decentralised data fusion (DDF)

**Sum fusion on the forest = the channel filter.** The v295 equality-forest sum (posterior = Σ over reached sources of (q_k, q_k μ_k)) is the information-form fusion of the decentralised data fusion literature; the device that makes it exact on a tree while avoiding double counting is the *channel filter* of Grime & Durrant-Whyte (1994), which tracks the information common to the two ends of each link and subtracts it. Dagan & Ahmed (2022, §II-B) restate its validity conditions exactly as this project uses them: undirected acyclic communication graph, full-rate communication, sequential processing, linear-Gaussian models. The forest cavity in `rollout_sum` (source-once, replacement messages) is the channel filter with the common information tracked implicitly by the tree. This is also why the earlier energy review found that equality-sum flooding on loops inflates precision ×875–×1582: the channel filter has no loop-safe version, which is the historical motivation for CI.

**Max-precision selection = scalar Covariance Intersection with the optimal weight.** Covariance Intersection (Julier & Uhlmann, 1997) fuses two estimates with unknown cross-correlation by P⁻¹ = ωP_a⁻¹ + (1−ω)P_b⁻¹, P⁻¹x = ωP_a⁻¹x_a + (1−ω)P_b⁻¹x_b, choosing ω to minimise a scalar criterion (trace or log-det); it is guaranteed consistent (never over-confident) for *any* correlation, which is what makes it loop-safe and duplicate-safe. For a scalar coordinate the fused precision ωp_a + (1−ω)p_b is linear in ω, so the minimum-variance ω is a vertex: the fused estimate is simply the more precise of the two, and for N sources the optimum over the simplex is the arg-max vertex. The same holds for Inverse Covariance Intersection (Noack, Sijs, Reinhardt & Hanebeck, 2017): in one dimension, p_ICI = p_a + p_b − 1/(ω/p_a + (1−ω)/p_b), maximised at a vertex, giving p = max(p_a, p_b) and the gain of the less precise estimate exactly 0. I checked both numerically (20,000 random pairs, grid over ω): worst deviation of (precision, mean) from max-precision selection 4e-16 (CI) and 1.5e-15 (ICI). Consequences:

- Coordinate-wise selection is not an ad-hoc heuristic; it is *the* conservative fusion rule that the DDF literature prescribes when correlations are unknown and the state is treated coordinate by coordinate. Reinhardt, Noack, Arambel & Hanebeck (2015) prove CI gives the minimal conservative bound when nothing is known about the correlation; Cros, Amblard, Prieur & Da Rocha (2023) prove the analogous optimality of Split CI when an uncorrelated component is known. The project's situation (two agents' block codes of the same wall are deterministic functions of the same geometry, i.e. fully correlated in the worst case) is exactly the "nothing known" case, so selection is the correct worst-case rule for the overlapped blocks; the energy review's point that overlapping observers are *not* independent (README §4.2) is settled in favour of selection, not of the sum.
- CI has an information-theoretic reading as a log-linear (Chernoff / geometric-mean) combination of the two densities (Hurley, 2001; the Kullback–Leibler average of Battistelli & Chisci, 2014 reduces to CI in the Gaussian case). With ω at a vertex the fused *energy* is one of the source energies: E_k(x) = ½ q_{i*k}(x − μ_{i*k})², i* = arg-max_i q_ik. This is a declared quadratic energy with an explicit, auditable selection rule, which is how I would satisfy README §3 constraint 2 ("explicit target energy and message semantics") in a paper, instead of calling the rule GBP (it is not sum-product or max-product on any Gaussian factor graph).
- Split CI (Julier & Uhlmann's variant that separates a known independent part from the unknown-correlation part; optimality proved by Cros et al. 2023) is the natural next rule if the project ever wants to *add* the independent part of two observers (e.g. coverage counts) while selecting on the correlated part. In 1-D SCI reduces to p = max(p_a^dep, p_b^dep) + p_a^ind + p_b^ind: selection on the dependent part, sum on the independent part. This is a principled version of the "coverage count as precision" idea from the representation review and it stays diagonal.

**Fast CI / conservative fusion.** The "fast CI" family replaces the ω-optimisation by closed-form or approximate weights (closed-form solutions for low dimensions: Reinhardt, Noack & Hanebeck, 2012); in the diagonal, per-coordinate case there is nothing to approximate (the optimum is a vertex), so any fast-CI variant that is exact in 1-D coincides with max-selection here. Bailey, Julier & Agamennoni (2012) generalise conservative fusion to non-Gaussian dependence with the geometric mean density; same vertex argument per coordinate.

### 2.2 Where it does *not* match the Gaussian literature

- CI discards information: when two observers are genuinely independent the sum would be right and selection loses up to half the Fisher information per coordinate. The report's own anatomy (D3-4b: pixels whose block winner did not observe them still reach recall .887 vs .919) shows that in this decoder the loss is small, but a reviewer will ask for the redundant-observation regime explicitly (two agents, same room, same walls): the energy review's T6 showed the sum's variance 1.35→0.064 at |S|=8; under max it stays 1/q_max. Whether the decoder benefits from the sum's over-confidence in that regime is an open empirical question the current panels do not isolate (D3-1b pools redundant and complementary blocks).
- CI's criterion is variance-minimisation of a *calibrated* estimator; here q is a learned confidence feature (Spearman 0.91 with coverage, N2-0), not a calibrated precision. Selection by an uncalibrated score is "most-confident-wins" rather than CI in the strict sense; the literature name is still right, but the consistency guarantee is only as good as q's monotonicity in true error, which has not been measured (recommend a reliability diagram of q vs block decoding error).

### 2.3 Multi-robot map merging and occupancy-grid fusion

- The classical per-cell rule for aligned occupancy grids is additive log-odds (Elfes 1989; Thrun 2003), i.e. the *sum* in information form; the day-1 reviews already cite these. The map-merging literature proper (Birk & Carpin 2006; Carpin 2008; the review by Yu, Fu, Gostar & Hu 2020) is almost entirely about estimating the alignment between grids; Birk & Carpin 2006 do not even specify a per-cell combination rule after alignment (checked in the PDF). So there is no established "max log-odds" or "most-confident-wins" rule for occupancy grids to cite as precedent; the honest statement is that selection has no precedent *for cells*, only for *estimates with unknown correlation* (§2.1) and for *replicated state* (§2.4).
- Ownership-style merging exists at the submap level: each robot owns the submaps/keyframes it created and merging is a union of owned pieces with pose-graph alignment (Voxgraph; Kimera-Multi; Swarm-SLAM; SMMR-Explore). The v342 "owned block" semantics is the cell-grid analogue of that, and the ACHORD field system (Saboia et al. 2022, §IV-A) merges its shared roadmap "favouring more recent data", i.e. an LWW rule, when robots meet. That is the closest field precedent for a selection rule and it is a version rule, not a precision rule (relevant to D6).

### 2.4 CRDTs, join-semilattices, gossip aggregation

- State-based CRDTs (Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero & Zawirski, 2011): a replicated object whose state forms a join-semilattice, whose updates are inflations, and whose merge is the join, converges under any delivery order, duplication and reordering (strong eventual consistency). The per-coordinate (p, η) state with the total order (p, version, [source id]) and merge = max is precisely an LWW-register in which the "timestamp" is the precision with version as tie-break (Shapiro et al. call the generic instance LWW-Register; with a numeric total order it is often called a max-register). The 64-vector of them is a product lattice, so the whole packet is a CRDT. The version vector (Parker et al. 1983) carried alongside is a second CRDT (pointwise max). Delta-state CRDTs (Almeida, Shoker & Baquero, 2018) formalise exactly what `eval_dynamic_v349.py` does with its change mask: send only the part of the state that changed since the last message on that link, with anti-entropy to recover losses; the "silent when nothing changed" rule and the per-link last-sent memory are the standard delta-CRDT mechanism. Terminology for the paper: "a 64-coordinate max-register CRDT with delta-state dissemination", with version vectors for bookkeeping.
- Gossip / in-network aggregation: an aggregate is *order- and duplicate-insensitive* (ODI) when it can be computed over arbitrary multipath topologies without double counting (Nath, Gibbons, Seshan & Anderson, 2004, synopsis diffusion); max is the canonical ODI synopsis, sum/count are not (they need sketches). Extrema propagation (Baquero, Almeida, Menezes & Jesus, 2012) is the classic trick that turns *sums* and network size into a max-aggregate via random exponentials, i.e. the way to get an idempotent, loop-safe estimate of a *count* (e.g. number of observers of a block) if the project ever wants additive statistics without leaving the semilattice. Push-sum / average consensus (Kempe et al. 2003; Xiao, Boyd & Lall 2005, already in the day-1 review) are the non-idempotent alternatives and need mixing time, not diameter.
- Max-consensus: on a fixed connected graph with synchronous updates x_i ← max over closed neighbourhood, every node holds the global maximum after at most D rounds, D = diameter, and the state is then a fixed point (Nejad, Attia & Raisch 2010 treat it in max-plus algebra, including switching topologies; Iutzeler, Ciblat & Jakubowicz 2012 analyse it under random wireless losses and show the convergence time is finite with probability one, with bounds on its mean and dispersion). The project's "idempotent beyond the diameter" (D3-0; `test_v347_max.py` L26, L50) is this result. With cavity exclusion (do not echo to the immediate sender) the bound still holds because shortest paths never backtrack. Caveats for the paper: (i) the bound is for connected graphs; R=8 rounds on an N=16 BFS forest can be smaller than the diameter, so what a node holds after 8 rounds is the max over its 8-hop ball, which is exactly the "actually reached union" target the project uses; (ii) with asynchronous or lossy delivery the convergence time is a random variable (Iutzeler et al.), which the evaluator has not exercised.

### 2.5 Are the three claims in D3-0 consistent with known results?

- *Exact on cycles.* Yes, in the sense of "the flooded join equals the centralised join" (ODI property, §2.4); this is a property of any semilattice and needs no Gaussian argument. It is *not* exactness of a Bayesian posterior: it is the CI-consistent (conservative) estimate. Implementation caveat (D2): belief candidates mix the unquantised own factor (L278) with quantised relays (L274, L291); the profile-4 precision codebook has 255 log-spaced levels on [1e-8, 128], a step ratio of ≈1.096, so rounding can lift a quantised copy above the unquantised original, and on a cycle a node can select its own echo. The test acknowledges this by excluding own-winner coordinates (L43-46). The CRDT path already joins the quantised own factor (L108); doing the same in `rollout_max` makes the semilattice exact on the wire without exception and removes a train/eval asymmetry between the three protocols in `eval_dynamic_v349.py` (crdt: quantised own; stateless sum and max: unquantised own in the belief).
- *Idempotent.* Yes (join-semilattice; duplicates and re-sends are absorbed). The relay in the evaluator even echoes a neighbour's information back to it (code review N6); harmless for correctness, costs bytes.
- *Equal to GBP when at most one source has non-prior precision.* Up to the prior term: sum gives P = q + |S|α, max gives q + α (α = 1e-4/(64N)); relative mean difference ≤ (|S|−1)α/q ≤ 1.5 % only at q = PRECISION_MIN = 1e-4 (N=16) and ≈ 1.5e-6 at q = 1. Not an error, but "equal" should read "equal up to the |S|-fold prior".
- *β→∞ limit of q^β-tempered averaging.* Correct (soft-max weights → arg-max); the same statement in CI language is "ω at a vertex".

### 2.6 Terminology recommendation for a paper

Use, in this order of precision: (1) "per-coordinate max-precision selection, which is coordinate-wise Covariance Intersection with the optimal weight (equivalently coordinate-wise ICI), i.e. conservative fusion under unknown correlation"; (2) "implemented as a state-based CRDT: a vector of max-registers (LWW-registers keyed by (precision, version)) with delta-state dissemination and version vectors"; (3) "disseminated by synchronous max-consensus (flooding), which converges in at most diameter rounds and is order- and duplicate-insensitive". Avoid: "GBP" for the max rule, "ownership-based fusion" (ownership is a property of the *code*, not of this rule; the rule selects among owners of the same block), and "extrema propagation" (that term denotes the specific sum-via-min-of-exponentials algorithm of Baquero et al., not plain max flooding).

## 3. Q2. End-to-end training through the aggregation operator; max vs sum/attention; asynchrony

### 3.1 What the collaborative-perception literature reports

- F-Cooper (Chen, Ma, Tang, Guo, Yang & Fu, SEC 2019) fuses voxel or spatial feature maps of two vehicles by element-wise maxout ("V_i = max(V_3^i, V_5^i)", §3.1), motivated by parameter-free prominence selection and small message size (features "one hundredth" of raw LiDAR). V2VNet (Wang et al., ECCV 2020) averages warped neighbour features inside a GNN with a ConvGRU; OPV2V's AttFuse (Xu et al., ICRA 2022) uses per-location self-attention; DiscoNet (Li et al., NeurIPS 2021) uses matrix-valued edge weights with early-fusion distillation; V2X-ViT (Xu et al., ECCV 2022) uses heterogeneous multi-agent attention; CoBEVT (Xu et al., CoRL 2022) fused axial attention; Where2comm (Hu et al., NeurIPS 2022) attention with spatial-confidence-driven message selection; CoCa3D (Hu et al., CVPR 2023) sends only high-confidence elements and fuses them with a *non-parametric point-wise maximum* (as described in the survey by Han et al. 2023, §III-A-3a).
- Numbers that matter for the project's question "max vs learned fusion": on V2XSet (V2X-ViT paper, Table 1, PointPillar backbone, 32× compression for all), Perfect setting AP@0.5/0.7: F-Cooper .840/.680, OPV2V-AttFuse .807/.664, V2VNet .845/.677, DiscoNet .844/.695, V2X-ViT .882/.712; Noisy setting (0.2 m / 0.2° pose noise + 100 ms delay): F-Cooper .715/.469, AttFuse .709/.487, V2VNet .791/.493, DiscoNet .798/.541, V2X-ViT .836/.614. So maxout is competitive with (and at AP@0.7 slightly above) mean and single-head attention under perfect conditions, and is beaten by the heavier attention models, most clearly under pose noise and delay. Han et al. (2023) summarise the same: on V2XSet "V2VNet and AttFusion are weaker than F-Cooper, which adopts maxout", while attention-based fusion "dominates" overall. The literature therefore supports "max is a strong, parameter-free fusion when the messages are spatially aligned and co-trained", and does not support "max is generally better than learned fusion"; the project's +2–5 pp (max over sum) is in line with the first statement and should not be over-generalised.
- Robustness to asynchrony is handled in that literature by *motion compensation*, not by the fusion operator: V2X-ViT's delay-aware positional encoding (Table 3: +0.011 to +0.020 AP@0.7 at 100–400 ms), SyncNet (Lei et al., ECCV 2022, "Latency-Aware Collaborative Perception") and CoBEVFlow (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2023, "Asynchrony-Robust Collaborative Perception via Bird's Eye View Flow") predict features forward in time. That machinery exists because the scenes are dynamic. The project's maps are static and the evidence is monotone, so "asynchrony" reduces to *stale versions*, for which a max-join over (precision, version) is the natural mechanism, and no prediction is needed. That is a real advantage of the setting, and also its limitation (D7).

### 3.2 Is there a known result that max fusion is bandwidth-friendly and duplicate-robust?

- Duplicate/robustness: yes, as a property of idempotent aggregation (ODI synopses, Nath et al. 2004; CRDT convergence under duplication and reordering, Shapiro et al. 2011; PointNet's "critical point set" argument that max-pooled features are unchanged by adding points inside the upper-bound shape, Qi et al. 2017, cited by Xu et al. 2019 §5.4). No collaborative-perception paper that I found makes the duplicate-robustness claim for maxout; it is inherited from distributed systems.
- Bandwidth: in the perception literature bandwidth gains come from what is sent (Who2com/When2com agent selection; Where2comm confidence-sparsified features; V2VNet variational compression; OPV2V's encoder-decoder with 4096× compression at ≈3 % AP loss) and not from the operator. The operator matters indirectly: because max is idempotent and monotone, a sender can transmit only coordinates that would *raise* the receiver's state (delta-state CRDT, Almeida et al. 2018), and relays can forward codebook values bit-exactly (no re-rounding, D3-0). Attribute the "11 %" / "diff" savings to delta-state dissemination enabled by the semilattice, not to "max fusion".

### 3.3 Co-adaptation of the decoder to the aggregator

- The report's observation (D3-2: a max-trained decoder collapses under sum, .65/.54 at t=5, and a sum-trained one is worse under crdt) is what the aggregator literature predicts. Xu, Hu, Leskovec & Jegelka (2019) rank sum > mean > max in expressive power and prove (Corollary 9) that the max aggregator captures only the underlying *set* of a multiset, ignoring multiplicities; the three aggregators therefore produce differently distributed features for the same inputs and a readout trained on one sees a covariate shift under another. Here the shift is concrete: under sum the log-variance channel is log 1/Σ_{k∈S}(q_k+α) and decreases with |S|, under max it is bounded below by log 1/(q_max+α); the hard8 mean is a blend under sum and a source value under max. Yehudai, Fetaya, Meirom, Chechik & Maron (2021) show that sum-aggregating GNNs fail to generalise when the local degree distribution shifts between train and test; |S| (number of reached sources) plays the role of degree here, and max is the aggregator that is invariant to it. So: co-adaptation is expected, "protocol must match training" is correct, and the right framing is aggregator/covariate shift, not a defect.
- A reviewer will still want the symmetric control the report already ran (sum-trained under crdt) reported in the same table as the max-trained one, plus a third arm trained with *randomised* fusion (max or sum per scene) to test whether a single decoder can be made aggregator-agnostic; the GNN literature suggests the answer is "partly, at a cost".

## 4. Q3. Dynamic memory protocol

### 4.1 Consistency with the delay-tolerant / intermittent-connectivity literature

- The memory protocol is store-carry-forward of a CRDT state: every node keeps the join of everything it has heard and re-floods it on every contact. That is epidemic routing (Vahdat & Becker 2000) applied to a mergeable state, and anti-entropy in the sense of Demers et al. (1987). In that literature it is a standing result that when the contact graph is connected at every step flooding already delivers everything, so buffering adds bytes (re-transmission of old state on new links) without information; buffering pays only across partitions. The report's D3-7 table (8 m: no gain, +33 % bytes; 5 m / 4 m: +4.5/+6.6 pp F1 and +8/+11 pp accuracy on the memory target for +36–46 % bytes) is exactly that pattern. Robotics confirms the same trade-off in the field: ACHORD (Saboia et al., RA-L 2022) buffers data during outages and makes robots "return to comms" when the buffer exceeds 300 KB, i.e. memory is what makes intermittent connectivity tolerable and bandwidth is the price; Amigoni, Banfi & Basilico (2017) survey the three regimes (continuous, recurrent/periodic, opportunistic connectivity), Hollinger & Singh (2012) give the periodic-connectivity theory, and the current exploration planners (Bramblett, Peddi & Bezzo 2022; Gao et al. 2022 "Meeting-Merging-Mission"; IR² by Tan et al. 2024/2025) plan rendezvous precisely because maps are only merged on contact.
- What a DTN reviewer will note is missing: the intermittent regime here is still *synchronous* (8 rounds per step on the current forest, every step) and lossless. Real contact graphs are asynchronous (pairwise contacts of varying duration), lossy and reorder packets. The semilattice guarantees correctness under all of that, but the *bytes* and the *delivery delay* change (Iutzeler et al. 2012 for max-consensus under losses; Almeida et al. 2018 for delta-CRDT anti-entropy cost). README §6 already lists duplicate packets, stale versions, graph changes and disconnected graphs as required tests; they have been run only as math tests on synthetic factors (`test_v347_max.py`), not through the learned code and the decoder.

### 4.2 Semantics of the memory (D6, D7)

- The join is "most precise factor ever heard" with version as a 1e-9 tie-break (L59-61). With a learned, non-monotone precision head a newer version of the same source can lose to an older one; the code review measured 57 % stale coordinates with an untrained model and the report bounds the effect for the trained model by own-F1 crdt .823 vs own K0 .833 (D3-2). In CRDT terms this is a max-register, not a per-source LWW register; the target definition (union of *latest* known versions, L142-146) and the docstring describe the latter. Two consistent fixes: (a) make the join lexicographic in (version, precision) per source, which needs a 4-bit source id per coordinate on the wire (review M5), or (b) keep the max-register and redefine the target as "union of the most-precise-heard version per source" (and say so). Since cumulative maps are monotone and the encoder's q is superlinear in coverage (N2-0), (a) should rarely change the winner, but the paper needs one or the other.
- Monotone evidence: the generator never retracts a cell (ternary max; verified by the code review, 0 non-monotone slices). Max-join is correct exactly in this case. For environments with changes (doors closing, moved furniture, the HouseExpo-plus-furniture data the user requested), a join cannot forget: this is the CRDT deletion problem (G-set vs 2P-set/LWW-element-set with tombstones in Shapiro et al. 2011). State the assumption explicitly and, if dynamic objects enter the data, switch the per-source element to a versioned replacement (per-source LWW with monotone version, which is still a CRDT) rather than a global max.

### 4.3 Is the byte accounting fair?

Fair in the comparison it makes (both diff-coded protocols billed by the same rule, D3-7 "1.33–1.46×"); a reviewer would object to the following before accepting absolute numbers:

1. Header. 8 B per packet on both diff protocols versus 44 B of overhead inside the sealed 172 B packet (148 B codec frame + 24 B session binding, per the code review). Either bill 44 B on the diff packets too or strip the binding from the reference; and state that transport/MAC headers (≥ 28 B for UDP/IPv4, ≥ 9 B for 802.15.4) are excluded. Application-layer accounting is normal in this literature (Where2comm reports feature bytes; ACHORD reports bzip2-compressed blobs), but it must be declared.
2. Per-coordinate cost. 2 B assumes one shared block exponent (profile-4); a joined packet can carry coordinates quantised at different sources with different exponents (review M4-iii). Either bill the exponent per coordinate (≈5 bits) or move to the k-bit line (D3-6: μ k bits + log-codebook precision k bits, no shared exponent), where 2k bits per coordinate is exact and the mask packet is 53.8 B at k=6.
3. Version vector. N bytes in every non-silent packet is the simplest correct choice; standard practice sends version vectors only when they change or as deltas (delta-CRDT ack maps, Almeida et al. 2018), and a version-only packet is billed 8+N here (correct per review M4-i). For N=16 the vector is 16 B against ≈22 B average payload, so it is not a rounding error; report the split.
4. Message count. Both protocols emit a packet on every directed edge every round (240 per step at N=16) and bill 0 B when silent. Real radios pay per transmission (beacons, ACKs, contention); the gossip literature reports messages and bytes separately. `packets_crdt` is already tracked; report it, and the fraction of silent packets.
5. The stateless control is link-stateful. "Diff-coded stateless flooding" keeps the last packet per directed edge; after a topology change new edges pay full packets (the evaluator does this correctly: unknown key ⇒ 64 changed). Call it "no per-source memory, per-link delta coding" to avoid the objection.
6. Missing baseline (the serious one). README §8 requires a strong lossless / incremental raw control for any byte claim. For the dynamic protocol that baseline is the incremental occupancy delta: on each contact send the set of cells newly known since the last exchange on that link (run-length or zlib coded; the set-reconciliation lower bound of Minsky, Trachtenberg & Zippel 2003 says cost ∝ |symmetric difference|, so a per-link delta is the fair raw protocol, not a full-map resend). Back-of-envelope at H128 (not a result): known fraction grows ≈0.8 pp per step per agent ≈ 130 new cells per agent per step; naive coordinates+value ≈ 15 bits/cell ≈ 250 B per agent-step, and a union delta must traverse up to N−1 forest edges, so a flooded raw delta is of order N·(N−1)·250 B ≈ 60 KB per step at N=16 before RLE (perhaps 15–20 KB after), while the crdt-diff bill is ≈ 0.12–0.15 × 240 × 172 B ≈ 5–6 KB per step. The learned protocol probably wins by 3–10× in bytes at F1 ≈ .93, but this has to be measured with the same per-link delta rule, both with and without compression, and reported next to the quality gap (lossless raw gives F1 = 1 on reached cells).

## 5. Q4. Is "dynamic fine-tune improves the static benchmark by +2.2 pp" plausible, and what is the right control?

- Plausible as a data effect. The static TRAIN distribution has three nearly coincident poses per agent and ≈5–6 % known cells; the dynamic slices span 3.6–11 % known, single-trajectory cumulative maps, six forests per world, and corridor/door transitions, i.e. far more diverse (pose, extent, support shape) inputs from the *same* generator. In the framework of Gontijo-Lopes, Smullin, Cubuk & Dyer (2020), augmentations help when they add *diversity* while keeping *affinity* (test distribution stays inside the training distribution); here affinity is high by construction (the static maps are the t=0 subset of the dynamic ones, verified by the code review) and diversity rises, which predicts a gain on the static panel. The learned-mapping literature generates its training data along exploration trajectories for the same reason (Shrestha, Tian, Feng & Dolan 2019; Ramakrishnan, Al-Halah & Grauman 2020), though neither reports a controlled diversity-vs-updates ablation that could be cited as the effect size.
- The planned control is confounded. dynft = 36 static epochs (one cosine schedule) + 21,312 further updates with a *fresh* 200-step warm-up and a new cosine at lr 1e-4 (`--init-from` loads weights only; review §5). The planned Phase-B "72-epoch static, single cosine" differs in three ways at once: data, learning-rate restart, and ordering. Warm restarts alone are known to change results (Loshchilov & Hutter 2017, SGDR), and the report notes the 36-epoch curves were still rising (D3-1). If long-72 reaches .93+ the report will conclude "more updates", if it stalls "diversity", but neither conclusion follows, because a 36+36 restart on static data is a third possibility.
- Required controls (one 7200 s batch, same seed 342202, all warm-started from max-s342202 `final.pt`, identical lr 1e-4 recipe and 21,312 updates): (a) static-ft: static TRAIN only; (b) dynft (exists); (c) mixed-ft: 50/50 static and dynamic slices (same update count). Then dynft − static-ft isolates data, mixed-ft tells whether the gain survives with less dynamic exposure, and long-72 (single cosine) answers the separate question of whether sequential training matches a single long schedule. Decision statistic: paired per-world cluster bootstrap on static R8 wall F1 (as in D3-1b), and the same on the never-selected dynamic panel (indices 8–15) which does not exist yet. Also report own K0 at t=5 of the dynamic episodes for (a), because a static-ft model should keep the zero-shot degradation (.937→.846) if the gain is really from dynamic data.
- Secondary confounds to disclose: dynft's FPR rose 0.4 pp (inside the 0.5 pp protection rule); the dynamic slices contain the same 296 TRAIN worlds, so the "diversity" is in observation patterns, not in geometry, which is a weaker form of diversity than HouseExpo would give.

## 6. Q5. The three things a strict reviewer would still demand (ordered)

1. **External validity: a second, non-synthetic world family with a sealed test split.** Everything (K0 .94, R8 .92–.94, bytes, memory) is on one corridor-spine generator with axis-aligned rooms, no furniture, no dynamic objects, and VALIDATION doubles as the development panel (README §5.1; the "fresh" panel indices 8–15 are from the same generator and have been looked at). HouseExpo (+ declared synthetic furniture) at native 128/256 with house-level splits is the minimum; without it none of the numbers transfer and the ownership/selection semantics (one scalar per 2.5 m block) has not been tested on irregular geometry.
2. **Internal validity: multi-seed, schedule-matched, pre-registered comparisons that are honoured.** Run-to-run noise is ±2 pp (four owned-DT reruns .828–.882); max vs sum was decided on two seeds with the pre-registered gate formally failed (FPR +0.5 pp on seed 1) and adopted by judgment; dynft is one seed against a confounded control (§5); the k-bit curve is one seed. A reviewer wants ≥3 seeds per arm, paired cluster bootstrap across worlds, the gates as registered, and the schedule-matched static-ft control.
3. **Protocol validity: the full communication story on general graphs with like-for-like bytes.** (i) general cyclic / disconnected / asynchronous / lossy graphs with the learned code (not only synthetic factors); (ii) bytes against the lossless incremental raw delta demanded by README §8 with identical header and per-link delta rules, plus message counts and the distributed cost of building the forest or, better, no forest at all (max needs none, which is the strongest argument for it and has not been exercised end-to-end); (iii) calibration evidence for q (selection is CI only if q orders error). Door/topology metrics stay a known weakness (door recall ≈ .3–.5) and should be reported, not fixed, for this claim.

## 8. Short answers to the five questions

1. Max-precision selection = coordinate-wise CI/ICI at the optimal weight (conservative fusion under unknown correlation) = a max-register CRDT per coordinate, flooded by max-consensus; the report's three claims hold with the stated caveats (prior counting, own-factor quantisation on cycles, "exact" meaning semilattice-exact / CI-consistent, not Bayesian-exact). Recommended name: "per-coordinate max-precision selection (scalar covariance intersection), implemented as a delta-state max-register CRDT".
2. Yes: F-Cooper/CoCa3D use (max)out fusion, OPV2V/V2VNet/DiscoNet/V2X-ViT/CoBEVT/Where2comm use mean/GNN/attention; benchmarks show maxout competitive under perfect alignment and behind attention under noise/delay; bandwidth in that literature comes from selection/compression, duplicate-robustness from idempotence (distributed-systems literature); co-adaptation follows from aggregator expressiveness/covariate-shift results (GIN; size generalisation).
3. Consistent with epidemic/anti-entropy/DTN results and with field systems; standard baselines are full-map-on-contact, per-link incremental delta (set-reconciliation bound), submap exchange, rendezvous planning; the accounting is fair as a ratio but needs the header/exponent/version-vector/message-count disclosures and the lossless incremental raw baseline required by README §8.
4. Plausible as diversity-with-affinity; the 72-epoch control is confounded by the warm restart; run static-ft with the identical recipe (plus mixed-ft) and use paired bootstrap.
5. Non-synthetic data with a sealed test split; multi-seed schedule-matched pre-registered comparisons; end-to-end protocol validity on general/asynchronous graphs with like-for-like bytes against the raw incremental baseline.
