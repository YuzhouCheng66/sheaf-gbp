# Multi-robot 2D occupancy-grid map merging and fusion (classical, learned, distributed), literature to 2026

Scope note for the writer. Our comparison point (from the assignment, NOT from the literature): 8-16 agents, common frame assumed known, each agent's map compressed to 64 scalars + 64 precisions (8 bit each), fused by per-coordinate max-precision selection (a lattice join, exact on any graph) over 8 synchronous rounds on a radio forest, decoded by a CNN; on HouseExpo floor plans at 128x128 (6-16 cm cells) the fused map's wall F1 vs the union of observations is 0.89-0.90; on a synthetic corridor-room family 0.94; messages 14-27 bytes. Every "relation to our comparison point" remark below is my inference, not a literature claim.

Evidence quality is marked per bullet: [fetched] = full page/abstract read via WebFetch; [snippet] = only a search-result snippet of the source was seen; [biblio] = only bibliographic metadata confirmed. ## Key Question 0 (context): What are the main classical and learned approaches to merging 2D occupancy grids, and how do the surveys organise them?

### Takeaway
The field is organised almost entirely around the relative-transform problem (registration), not around the cell-fusion rule: the surveys classify methods as probabilistic/pose-graph, optimisation-of-overlap (Birk & Carpin), image-registration/feature-based (SIFT/ORB/KAZE, ROS multirobot_map_merge), and Hough-spectrum (Carpin 2008), while the actual per-cell combination is treated as a one-line rule (odds product, max, or lowest-entropy cell). Learned work to 2026 either (a) learns the registration+fusion end to end for pairs of grids (van Kempen 2023), (b) learns messages/latents for exploration rather than for map reconstruction (Ma 2024, Rajasekar 2026), or (c) uses GNNs for task allocation on top of a conventionally fused shared map (NeuralCoMapping 2022).

### Inferences
- The literature's "map merging" problem is dominated by transform estimation; once the transform is known, every classical paper reduces fusion to a fixed per-cell rule (odds product, max, or keep-the-lower-entropy cell). The one survey that treats fusion explicitly (Yu et al. 2020) devotes three equations to it and no evaluation.
- Learned methods to 2026 do not target the known-frame fusion problem itself: van Kempen 2023 learns registration+fusion for pairs (2 maps); NeuralCoMapping fuses conventionally and learns allocation; 4CNet and DistGP learn map prediction/representation, not multi-agent combination of grids.

### Gaps
- The exact formula and original experiments of Birk & Carpin 2006 and Carpin 2008 were not readable (403 on both PDFs); acceptance-index numbers from those originals are missing.
- Saeedi et al. 2016 content was not reviewed (only bibliographic data confirmed).
- No source found that applies Siamese/transformer registration to 2D occupancy grids in 2024-2025 (searched; closest is FRAME, arXiv 2404.18006, which is for 3D point-cloud maps).

## Key Question 1: With a known common frame, what is the state of the art for fusing partial occupancy grids from several robots, how close do fused maps get to the union of observations, and is it a solved problem?

### Takeaway
Under a known common frame the literature treats cell-wise fusion as essentially closed: the standard rules are multiplicative odds (log-odds sum) for probabilistic grids, element-wise max for ROS trinary grids, and lower-entropy-wins for mixed-quality maps; no paper found reports "distance to the union of observations" because with a known frame and lossless exchange the fused map IS the union. The remaining difficulty is explicitly located elsewhere: bandwidth/centralisation of grid exchange, heterogeneous map quality and resolution, dynamic environments, and the fact that the fusion rule only matters at the 0.001-0.02 accuracy level once sensor models are matched (Berlenko & Krinkin 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Known-pose fusion rule (Burgard et al. 2000 lineage): per-robot odds multiplied and converted back to probability, Eqs. 2-4 of Yu et al. 2020. [fetched] — [Yu et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7730201)
- ROS multirobot_map_merge fuses by `cv::max` over warped grids with unknown = -1, so known beats unknown and the higher occupancy value wins in overlaps; the composition is order-independent. [fetched source] — [grid_compositor.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hrnr/m-explore/master/map_merge/src/combine_grids/grid_compositor.cpp)
- Sunil et al. 2023 fuse overlapping cells with a product-form Bayesian rule p(G) = AB / (AB + (1-A)(1-B)) and state the result is "independent of the order of merging"; they contrast it with log-odds addition and lookup tables. [fetched] — [PMC10055820](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10055820/)
- Chen & Chan 2023 fuse overlapping cells in log-odds form but then compute per-cell entropy and keep the lower-uncertainty cell (an "entropy filter"). [fetched] — [PMC10458590](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10458590/)
- Berlenko & Krinkin, "Bayesian log-odds vs Dempster's rule for 2D occupancy grids" (title paraphrased), arXiv Feb 2026: with per-observation sensor models matched via the pignistic transform, Bayesian log-odds fusion is favoured in 15/15 runs (p = 3.1e-5) but absolute differences are only 0.001-0.022; an apparent +12 % boundary-sharpness advantage of belief functions reversed to a 22 % (dynamic baseline) / 29 % (noisy sensor) deficit after matching. Multi-robot simulation: 3 robots, 20x20 m at 0.1 m resolution (200x200 cells), 1 room, 5 static + 3 dynamic obstacles, 90-ray lidar (sigma 0.03 m), simulated odometry drift, maps aligned by pose-graph optimisation at 2.5 m rendezvous then fused cell-wise; metrics cell accuracy (+0.0105 and +0.0094 for Bayesian, TOST-equivalent at delta 0.02), boundary sharpness (+0.0701 / +0.0628), map entropy (-0.0261 / -0.0256); no F1/IoU; "multi-robot real-data conditions used scan-splitting with ideal alignment, not true multi-robot deployment". [fetched] — [arXiv 2602.18872](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18872)
- Dempster-Shafer grid fusion appears in older cooperative mapping (dynamic region decomposition, two simulated robots, range readings fused with DS rules). [snippet] — [robot.sia.cn article 1011](https://robot.sia.cn/en/article/id/1011)
- Merging a lower-quality map into a higher-quality one can degrade the latter (motivation for CNN map-quality assessment). [snippet] — [Andersone 2020](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2020/91755/91755.pdf)
- Different-resolution merging remains "highly challenging"; most methods handle two maps at a time. [fetched] — [Yu et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7730201)
- Sharing occupancy grids is "too hefty in a large environment"; merging on every robot without infrastructure is "unrealistic". [fetched] — [Kegeleirs 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frobt.2021.618268/full)
- Lajoie 2022 lists dynamic environments (moving objects, including other robots), resilient communication (delays, dropouts, out-of-sequence messages), Byzantine poisoning of map merging, and scalability to large teams as open problems; "The days of raw sensor data transmission are over." [fetched] — [arXiv 2108.08325](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.08325)
- NeuralCoMapping (known poses, centralised): all robots' depth images are fused into one shared 2D occupancy map; robots occupy cells that are marked occupied and reopened when the robot moves away. [fetched] — [ar5iv 2203.16319](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.16319)
- Asgharivaskasi & Atanasov 2024 assume known robot poses and a fully connected graph; their consensus-constrained updates "resemble Bayes rule with one-hop prior averaging". [fetched] — [arXiv 2402.08867](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08867v1)

### Inferences
- With a known frame and full-grid exchange, max/OR fusion of trinary grids or log-odds summation of probabilistic grids reproduces the union of observations exactly up to the rule's handling of conflicts; this is why no paper measures "distance to the union": the quantity is trivially 1.0 unless maps are compressed, dropped, or mis-registered. The absence of such a metric in the literature is itself a finding: our 0.89-0.90 wall-F1-vs-union number has no published analogue because prior work does not compress before fusing.
- The literature's residual difficulty under a known frame is (i) communication cost of grids (Kegeleirs 2021, Lajoie 2022, survey 2503.07278), (ii) conflict handling between maps of different quality/resolution (Chen & Chan's entropy filter, Andersone 2020, Yu 2020), and (iii) dynamics; the per-cell rule choice is now shown to matter only at the ~1-2 % accuracy level (Berlenko & Krinkin 2026).
- ROS map_merge's cv::max rule is a join on the totally ordered value set {-1 < 0 ... 100}; it is idempotent, commutative and associative, so it is already a (trivial) lattice join. No source frames it this way.

### Gaps
- No paper found that quantifies fused-map quality relative to the union of observations under a known frame; all known-frame papers report coverage/time or SLAM accuracy instead.
- No source found evaluating log-odds fusion with 8-16 robots on floor-plan datasets; the largest known-frame cell-fusion experiment with per-cell metrics found is 3 robots at 0.1 m (Berlenko & Krinkin 2026).

## Key Question 2: Which works evaluate fusion quality per pixel (F1/IoU/accuracy/acceptance index of cells) rather than coverage/time, and what numbers do they report at what resolution?

### Takeaway
Per-pixel evaluation of merged grids is rare and almost always uses the Birk-Carpin acceptance index (agreeing / (agreeing + disagreeing) overlapping cells) on 2-6 maps at 4-10 cm cells, with reported values 0.90-0.99; IoU/F1 of occupied cells against ground truth is essentially absent from the merging literature (one semantic mIoU in DSS-MAP 2026; cell accuracy deltas in Berlenko & Krinkin 2026). Learned exploration papers report coverage ratio (92-99 %) and steps, not per-pixel map fidelity.

### Cited Findings

- Chen & Chan 2023 (Gazebo + Gmapping; maps at 20, 20, 10, 20 cells/m, i.e. 5-10 cm cells; LiDAR noise sigma 0.005 m for three maps and 0.05 m for one): acceptance index omega with merge threshold 0.95; baseline (Mills/SIFT) omega = 0.966292 / 0.935030 / 0.903026 on three sets with only set 1 merging successfully; proposed pre-processing omega = 0.992072 / 0.989327 / 0.979052 (+2.67 %, +5.81 %, +8.42 %), all merged. Scenario 2 (three maps at 20 cells/m, university floor plan): omega = 0.984169 and 0.984952. Comparison target is the other local maps (overlap agreement), judged also by human inspection; no ground-truth IoU. [fetched] — [PMC10458590](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10458590/)

## Key Question 3: Which works limit communication (bandwidth, message size, number of rounds) while fusing, and what trade-off do they measure?

### Takeaway
Communication-limited fusion is split between (a) consensus/gossip schemes that exchange whole maps or per-cell distributions but only locally and prove convergence (Ramachandran/Kakish/Berman T-RO 2020; Shirsat et al. RA-L 2022; Asgharivaskasi & Atanasov 2024; DSS-MAP 2026) and (b) compression schemes that cut bytes by 1-3 orders of magnitude but change the task (exploration or navigation) rather than reporting fused-map fidelity (Ma et al. 2024: 256-byte learned messages, ~99 % less traffic, 2.4 % longer to 7.2 % shorter paths; Psomiadis et al. 2024/25: 24-bit abstractions on a 64x64 grid; marsupial team 2025: 300x compression). No work found reports a bytes-per-message versus per-pixel-fidelity curve for grid fusion.

### Cited Findings
Consensus / gossip fusion:
- Ramachandran, Kakish, Berman, "Information correlated Levy walk exploration and distributed mapping using a swarm of robots", IEEE T-RO 2020 (arXiv 1903.04836): each robot broadcasts its stored map to robots within range b_r (maps carry no robot identity); per-cell update P_j^i(k+1) = u(own range update) x product over neighbours and self of P_j^n(k)^{a_in(k)} with doubly stochastic weights (0.5 self / 0.5 neighbour; a_ii = 1 with no neighbours), one neighbour merged per time step; Theorem 1 proves exponential convergence of all robots' maps to the true map if the union of communication graphs is connected over some interval and the set of informative steps is finite; simulations with 1, 5, 10, 20, 50 robots in environments of 16x16 m to 90x80 m (Freiburg campus), hardware with TurtleBot3 Burgers; metrics are percentage of domain mapped after fixed time and map entropy (values in figures only); cell size and communication range not stated in text. [fetched] — [ar5iv 1903.04836](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.04836)
- Shirsat, Mishra, Zhang, Berman, "Probabilistic Consensus on Feature Distribution for Multi-robot Systems with Markovian Exploration Dynamics", IEEE RA-L 2022 (arXiv 2202.03327): robots exchange per-cell feature PMFs only when at the same node (communication radius < 0.5 x node spacing); Chernoff fusion f ∝ ∏_b [f^b]^{ω(a,b)} with Metropolis weights, then thresholded to occupancy 0.8 / 0.2; 5.6x5.6 m domain, 8x8 = 64 nodes at 0.7 m; numerical simulations with 4 robots and Monte Carlo at N = 4, 8, 12, 16; SITL with 2 quadrotors; Hellinger distance to ground truth reaches 0 by 500 s (robot 4 at ~0.07 at 240 s); for N = 16 consensus converges in ~438 s vs ~830 s without consensus (about 2x); claims to preserve "the required communication bandwidth by relying only on local inter-robot communication" but gives no byte figures. [fetched] — [ar5iv 2202.03327](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.03327); [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10344131)
- Asgharivaskasi & Atanasov, "Distributed Optimization with Consensus Constraint for Multi-Robot Semantic Octree Mapping", arXiv Feb 2024: per-cell multi-class log-odds, semantic octree compression; each robot publishes its octree about once per second and each arrival triggers one update; octree uses more than 4x less bandwidth than a uniform grid (absolute packet sizes not given on page); 6 ClearPath Huskies in Unity, known poses, fully connected graph; metric is total divergence between local maps (decreases over time, no values); no accuracy/IoU. [fetched] — [arXiv 2402.08867](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08867v1)
- DSS-MAP (CDSR 2026): per-voxel evidence counts, compressed incremental differences, credibility-weighted gossip consensus; 98.5 % reduction in transmitted data vs raw fusion; semantic mIoU 24.59 %; ROS 2/Gazebo; targets severe bandwidth constraints and intermittent connectivity. [snippet] — [CDSR 2026 paper 126](https://www.avestia.com/CDSR2026_Proceedings/files/paper/CDSR_126.pdf)
- Mahajan, Raghavan, Johansson, "Finite-Sample Conformal Coverage Recovery via Fusion under Degraded Local Guarantees in Occupancy Map Estimation", arXiv Jul 2026: robots exchange only "lightweight scalar e-values" with neighbours; fused set-valued map reaches target coverage regardless of graph topology or noise distribution; denser topologies shrink the unclassified fraction; no numbers on abstract page. [fetched abstract] — [arXiv 2607.14906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14906)

Compression / learned messages:
- Ma, Liang, Cao, Tan, Sartoretti, "Privileged Reinforcement and Communication Learning for Distributed, Bandwidth-limited Multi-robot Exploration", arXiv Jul 2024 (NUS): each robot broadcasts a learned 1x64 float32 message (256 bytes) every step; a cooperation decoder uses the robot's own message as cross-attention query with others' messages as keys/values (arbitrary team size); trained with 4 robots, tested with 4 and 8; 4,000 training and 100 test 100x100 m dungeon maps, sensor range 20 m, graph nodes 4 m apart; Table 1 traffic: 4 robots baseline upload 656.2 MB / ours 5.7 MB, download 1791.1 / 17.2 MB; 8 robots 554.7 / 4.5 MB and 3303.1 / 32.0 MB (claimed up to 99.2 % savings); travel distance 205.7 vs 200.8 (4 robots, +2.4 %) and 168.9 vs 182.2 (8 robots, -7.2 %); a variant that also shares full maps reaches 182.2 / 150.1; no explicit cross-agent consistency mechanism beyond parameter sharing and a privileged critic; no map-fidelity metric. [fetched] — [arXiv 2407.20203](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.20203v1)
- Psomiadis, Maity, Tsiotras, "Multi-agent Task-Driven Exploration via Intelligent Map Compression and Sharing", arXiv Mar 2024 (v2 Aug 2025): 64x64 grid; Sensors send multi-resolution abstractions (mean + variance per compressed cell, chosen from a codebook of 10) with n_m = 24 bits per abstraction plus n_a = 4 bits (variance omission halves n_m); the Actor decodes by a convex program bounding per-cell uncertainty and plans with Dijkstra; 1 Actor + 3 Sensors (Scenario 1) or 1 Sensor (Scenario 2), 100 runs each; Scenario 1 r_cost 0.30-0.31 (about 70 % cost reduction vs uninformed) at r_bits 0.98-1; Scenario 2 proposed r_cost 0.72 at r_bits 0.78 vs fully-informed 0.67 at r_bits 1 and predefined-AS 0.87 at r_bits 0.16. [fetched] — [arXiv 2403.14780](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.14780v2)
- Larsson & Maity, "Communication-aware Hierarchical Map Compression of Time-Varying Environments for Mobile Robots", arXiv Apr 2025: multi-resolution hierarchical encoder trading map distortion against description size (bandwidth to transmit to other agents); no fusion across agents stated; no numbers on abstract page. [fetched abstract] — [arXiv 2504.10751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10751)
- Marsupial ground-aerial team (arXiv Sep 2025): task-driven point-cloud/occupancy compression reports 300x compression vs 10x-80x in prior methods, for bandwidth-limited, high-latency links. [snippet] — [arXiv 2509.07655](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07655v1)
- Bayer & Faigl (Autonomous Robots 2026): coordinate by sharing only positions, arguing map exchange is infeasible on very low-bandwidth links. [snippet] — [comrob AURO 2026](https://comrob.fel.cvut.cz/papers/auro26mre.pdf)
- Achey, Crockett, Heckman, Hayes, "RF-Modulated Adaptive Communication Improves Multi-Agent Robotic Exploration", arXiv Feb 2026: transmission location chosen from signal strength and payload size; 480+ simulations in three cave-inspired environments; up to 58 % less distance and 52 % faster exploration than rendezvous/minimum-signal baselines; payload sizes and map-quality not reported on abstract page. [fetched abstract] — [arXiv 2602.12074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12074)
- NeuralCoMapping: inter-robot bandwidth about 10-20 KB (poses + depth back-projection into a shared map). [fetched] — [ar5iv 2203.16319](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.16319)
- Anderson et al. (older) reduce occupancy-grid resolution to cut wireless traffic. [snippet] — via [arXiv 2602.12074 search context](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12074)
- Lajoie 2022: the communication bottleneck is usually inter-robot loop-closure data; compression options include self-organising maps, vocabulary indexes, distributed inverted indexes, landmark removal, feature coding, sharing only novel measurements, object descriptors; budget-aware exchange at encounters (Giamou et al. 2018; Tian et al. 2018-2020); extreme links: underwater acoustics (Paull et al. 2014/2015) and low-bandwidth long-range radio (Schulz et al. 2019); cloud offloading reached 256 robots (Zhang et al. 2018) but bandwidth becomes the limit. [fetched] — [ar5iv 2108.08325](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.08325)
- Kegeleirs 2021: consensus adds delay and data exchange; divide-and-conquer may reduce it (Yazdani et al. 2019); sparse swarm distributions limit inter-robot communication (Tarapore et al. 2020). [fetched] — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frobt.2021.618268/full)

### Inferences
- Measured trade-offs in the literature are of three kinds: (1) consensus convergence time vs number of robots/rounds (Shirsat: ~2x faster at N = 16 with consensus; Ramachandran: exponential convergence under joint connectivity), (2) bytes vs task cost (Ma: ~99 % fewer bytes for -7 % to +2 % path length; Psomiadis: r_bits vs r_cost), and (3) bytes vs structural fidelity (Asgharivaskasi: >4x fewer bytes with octrees; DSS-MAP: 98.5 % fewer bytes at 24.6 % semantic mIoU). None reports bytes vs occupied-cell F1 of a fused grid.
- Message sizes in the closest learned-message work are 256 bytes (64 x float32) per robot per step (Ma 2024), i.e. about 10-18x larger than our 14-27 byte messages, and are used for policy conditioning rather than reconstructing the map.
- The consensus schemes (averaging in the log/odds domain with doubly stochastic or Metropolis weights) are not idempotent: repeated or duplicated messages bias the estimate, which is why they need doubly stochastic weights, one-neighbour-per-step ordering (Ramachandran Assumption 2) or Metropolis weights (Shirsat). A max-precision join avoids this requirement; no source found makes this comparison explicitly.

### Gaps
- No source found that reports a message-size versus fused-map-fidelity curve for 2D occupancy grids, nor any work using a fixed small number of synchronous rounds (e.g. 8) as the communication budget.
- Absolute packet sizes for Asgharivaskasi & Atanasov 2024 and Shirsat 2022 were not available on the pages read.
- CRDT / join-semilattice framings of grid fusion: searched ("CRDT map merging robots lattice join monotone occupancy") and found no robotics source; the closest is the cv::max compositor in ROS map_merge and the reversible-merge framework of Andersone (2012, Latvian journal), which stresses reversibility, i.e. the opposite of a monotone join. [snippet] — [Andersone 2012](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.2478/v10312-012-0003-5.pdf)

## Key Question 4: Is there prior work that fuses learned low-dimensional map codes (latents) across robots rather than raw grids, and how is cross-agent consistency handled?

### Takeaway
Only two works found exchange learned latent codes of occupancy maps between robots, and neither fuses them into a reconstructed map: Ma et al. 2024 exchange 64-float learned messages consumed by a cross-attention policy (consistency only via shared network weights), and Rajasekar & Ratnoo 2026 compress maps with an autoencoder and combine shared data by a trust-weighted consensus inside an RL policy. No work found decodes fused latents back into a grid, attaches per-coordinate precisions to latents, or guarantees order/duplicate-independence of latent fusion.

### Cited Findings
- Ma et al. 2024: message = output of each robot's "state decoder", 1x64 float32 (256 bytes), broadcast every step; fused on the receiver by cross-attention (own message as query, others' as keys/values) into a "cooperative feature"; homogeneous policy with parameter sharing; privileged critic during training; no explicit consistency mechanism and no map reconstruction from messages. [fetched] — [arXiv 2407.20203](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.20203v1)
- Rajasekar & Ratnoo, "Latent Space Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Robot Exploration", arXiv Jan 2026: autoencoders compress "high-fidelity occupancy maps into latent state vectors"; a hierarchical deep RL framework for decentralised coordination includes a "weighted consensus mechanism" with a tuneable trust parameter controlling reliance on shared data; trained on Perlin-noise procedural environments (asteroid fields, caves, forests); abstract claims scaling with agent count, generalisation, and resilience under limited communication, with no numbers, robot counts, latent sizes, or map-quality metrics on the abstract page. [fetched abstract] — [arXiv 2601.01139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01139)
- DistGP (ICRA 2026): a shared sparse-GP map whose factorisation mirrors the robots is trained by distributed asynchronous Gaussian belief propagation; cross-agent consistency comes from the GBP message passing; robust to sparse communication; no message-size figures on the abstract page. [fetched abstract] — [arXiv 2603.07351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07351)
- Mahajan et al. 2026: scalar e-values (not latents) exchanged and fused with an "uncertainty-attenuated fusion operator"; consistency guaranteed via conformal coverage bounds regardless of topology. [fetched abstract] — [arXiv 2607.14906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14906)
- van Kempen et al. 2023: fusion of two evidential OGMs by a DNN that also outputs first- and second-order uncertainty; the exchanged object is the full grid, not a latent. [fetched abstract] — [arXiv 2304.03578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03578)
- Tzes et al. 2022 (GNN active information acquisition): inter-robot aggregation of learned features is performed by a Distributed Kalman Filter. [snippet] — [arXiv 2209.12091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.12091)
- Lajoie 2022 lists learned/compact descriptors shared between robots in C-SLAM (self-organising maps, Saeedi et al. 2011b and Best & Hollinger 2020; object-based descriptors, Choudhary et al. 2017b and Ramtoula et al. 2020) but these are place-recognition descriptors, not map codes to be fused. [fetched] — [ar5iv 2108.08325](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.08325)
- Psomiadis et al. 2024/25 and Larsson & Maity 2025 compress grids with hand-designed multi-resolution abstractions (mean + variance per block; 24-bit abstractions), not learned latents; the receiver decodes by a convex program bounding per-cell uncertainty. [fetched] — [arXiv 2403.14780](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.14780v2); [arXiv 2504.10751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10751)
- Searches for "learned occupancy map fusion uncertainty-aware neural multi-robot latent map sharing" and "learned map compression latent occupancy grid multi-robot" returned no additional learned-latent fusion work; results were classical merging, GP/Hilbert-map fusion (Bayesian Committee Machines noted in a 2024 thesis related-work section), and the items above. [search] — [arXiv 2407.10383 thesis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.10383)

### Inferences
- Cross-agent consistency in the two latent-sharing works is implicit (shared weights; trust-weighted averaging) and not exact: averaging latents is order-dependent under message loss/duplication and neither paper reports a reconstruction metric. Our per-coordinate max-precision join (idempotent, commutative, associative) addresses a problem the existing latent-sharing literature does not pose.
- Attaching explicit per-coordinate precisions to a latent code, so that fusion becomes a monotone selection rather than an average, has no published analogue in the sources found; the nearest conceptual relatives are the per-cell mean+variance abstractions of Psomiadis et al. (hand-designed, decoded by optimisation) and the evidential second-order uncertainty output of van Kempen et al. (per cell, not per latent coordinate).

### Gaps
- Full texts of Rajasekar & Ratnoo 2026 and DistGP 2026 were not read; latent dimensionality, robot counts, message sizes and any map-fidelity numbers are unknown.
- No source found that (i) decodes fused latents into a grid and scores it against ground truth or union, (ii) uses 8-bit quantised latents and precisions, or (iii) studies the effect of the number of synchronous fusion rounds on reconstruction quality.
- The term "MAP fusion with uncertainty" from the assignment did not resolve to a specific paper in searches; the closest matches are van Kempen et al. 2023 (uncertainty-aware learned fusion of evidential grids) and the 2018 hierarchical probabilistic fusion framework for 3-D occupancy maps (Yue et al., IEEE Sensors Journal; Mahalanobis evaluation of candidate transforms) [snippet] — [BIT record](https://pure.bit.edu.cn:443/en/publications/hierarchical-probabilistic-fusion-framework-for-matching-and-merg).
