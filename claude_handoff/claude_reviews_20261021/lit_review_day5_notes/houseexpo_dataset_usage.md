## Q0. What HouseExpo contains (size, source, format, resolution, license, caveats)

### Takeaway
HouseExpo (Li et al., IROS 2020; arXiv 1903.09845) is 35,126 2-D wall-only floor plans (252,550 rooms, mean 7.14 rooms/house) derived from 45,622 SUNCG 3-D house models, stored as JSON polylines in metres (plus PNG renders), MIT-licensed, with an OpenAI-Gym simulator (PseudoSLAM) whose metre-to-pixel scale is a user parameter (official benchmark configs use 16 px/m for exploration and 33 px/m for obstacle avoidance). The paper itself reports no map-accuracy benchmark beyond a one-map 98.2 % IoU of PseudoSLAM vs gMapping at an unstated resolution.

### Cited Findings
- Size: "35,126 2D floor plans including 252,550 rooms in total", with a room-category label per room; mean 7.14 / median 7.0 rooms per house — [GitHub README](https://github.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo); [arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09845).
- Count discrepancy across versions: the arXiv v1 abstract and Papers-with-Code entry say 35,357 floor plans; v4 and the repo say 35,126 — [arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09845v1.pdf); [Papers with Code](https://cs.paperswithcode.com/paper/houseexpo-a-large-scale-2d-indoor-layout). The Azizi et al. floor-plan paper repeats both numbers inconsistently (35,357 in Sec. 3.1, 35,126 in Sec. 3.1.1) — [ar5iv 2012.04735](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.04735).
- Source and construction: built from k = 45,622 SUNCG 3-D models; Algorithm 1 slices at ground-plane height 0.01 m and door-plane height 0.2 m, uses n = 500 cell-checking and m = 100 connectivity samples, fills closed cells smaller than 2 m^2 as obstacles, removes duplicates by pixel difference, and human labelling removed isolated garages, object-only scenes and odd layouts and added walls to open houses — [ar5iv 1903.09845](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.09845).
- Storage format: JSON per house with `id`, `room_num`, `bbox` (min/max), `verts` (x,y vertex list in metres), `room_category` (category -> bounding box); line segments are stored in metres relative to the house centroid; PNG renders of all plans are on a Google Drive link — [GitHub README](https://github.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo); [ar5iv 1903.09845](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.09845).
- Room categories are inherited from SUNCG; Azizi et al. reduced them "from 25 to 10" types, implying ~25 SUNCG categories in the raw labels — [ar5iv 2012.04735](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.04735).
- Size comparison claimed by the authors: "the largest 2D floor plan datasets are MIT campus dataset and KTH campus dataset with the size of 775 and 165 floor plans" — [ar5iv 1903.09845](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.09845).
- PseudoSLAM: OpenAI-Gym-compatible; outputs a 3-state (free/obstacle/uncertain) occupancy grid in the same format as gMapping/hector_slam; user-set Gaussian laser-range noise and Gaussian registration noise (x, y, theta); obstacles can be static or dynamic; "IoU of 98.2 % against gMapping (in Stage) on one floor plan, with walls treated as bounding boxes"; resolution for that comparison not stated — [ar5iv 1903.09845](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.09845).
- Config parameters exposed in `pseudoslam/envs/config.yaml`: `meter2pixel` (px per metre; "higher = more precise but slower"), `laser.range` (m), `laser.fov` (deg), `laser.resolution` (deg), `laser.noiseSigma`, `slamError.linear` (px), `slamError.angular` (deg), `robotRadius`, `stepLength`, `stateSize`, `mode` (0 exploration / 1 navigation), `obstacle.number/size` — [GitHub README](https://github.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo).
- Official exploration benchmark config (`experiments/config_autonomous_exploration.yaml`): meter2pixel = 16 (6.25 cm/cell), laser range 2 m, FOV 180 deg, 1 deg angular resolution, noise sigma 0, slamError 0/0, robot radius 0.2 m, step 1 m / 30 deg, state 4 x 4 m (= 64 x 64 px), mode 0, no added obstacles — [raw config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo/master/experiments/config_autonomous_exploration.yaml).
- Official obstacle-avoidance config (`experiments/config_obstacle_avoidance.yaml`): meter2pixel = 33 (~3.0 cm/cell), laser range 5 m, FOV 180 deg, 0.7 deg resolution, noise 0, 5 obstacles of 0.4-1.0 m, step 0.3 m / 10 deg, state 3 x 3 m, mode 1 — [raw config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo/master/experiments/config_obstacle_avoidance.yaml).
- The `experiments/` folder ships `map_id_train.txt` and `map_id_test.txt` plus the two configs — [GitHub experiments dir](https://github.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo/tree/master/experiments).
- Paper's exploration experiment: 24,588 training / 10,538 test houses (sums to 35,126); empty houses; local 4 m state; 200 steps; reward proportional to newly discovered area; baseline = uniform random policy; metric = explored area within 200 steps plotted per house (Fig. 11) with no numeric averages reported — [ar5iv 1903.09845](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.09845).
- Paper's obstacle-avoidance experiment: PPO, 3 conv + 128-unit LSTM, laser 5 m, state 3 m, reward -1 on collision else 0.9 x new area + 0.1 x forward; real TurtleBot test in a 3.2 x 5.5 m room, 5 scenes x 10 episodes: collisions 2/0/2/0/2, mean trajectory length 18.41/17.98/18.08/15.62/14.67 (units not stated) — [ar5iv 1903.09845](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.09845).
- License: MIT (repo); the arXiv page only carries the arXiv distribution licence for the paper — [GitHub README](https://github.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09845).
- Venue: IEEE/RSJ IROS 2020, pp. 5839-5846, DOI 10.1109/IROS45743.2020.9341284 — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9341284/); authors Tingguang Li, Danny Ho, Chenming Li, Delong Zhu, Chaoqun Wang, Max Q.-H. Meng — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09845).
- Repo caveats: tested only with Python 3.6 / TensorFlow 1.15 / Ubuntu 16.04; dynamic obstacles "not yet integrated"; Gym registration requires editing the Gym package — [GitHub README](https://github.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo).
- Semantic Scholar lists 77 citing papers for arXiv 1903.09845 (fetched 2026-10-23) — [S2 API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:1903.09845/citations?fields=title,year,venue,externalIds,citationCount&limit=500).

### Inferences
- Because the plans are stored as metric polylines, every downstream paper chooses its own rasterisation; the dataset has no canonical cells-per-metre. The two official configs (16 and 33 px/m) bracket the 6-16 cm/cell of our setup (16 px/m = 6.25 cm/cell exactly matches our finest end).
- The 98.2 % PseudoSLAM-vs-gMapping IoU is a simulator-fidelity check on a single map, not a learned-prediction or fusion benchmark, and should not be compared to prediction IoUs.
- The dataset contains walls only (no furniture); papers that need clutter add it synthetically (PseudoSLAM `obstacle` parameter, GAN-SLAM's modified simulator).

### Gaps
- Room-area/house-area statistics appear only in Fig. 3 of the paper; no text numbers were found, so the physical extent of a "typical" HouseExpo house (needed to convert a 128 x 128 house-fitted grid into cm/cell) could not be verified.
- Current availability status of the upstream SUNCG dataset (relevant to redistribution/licensing of derived data) was not checked in this session.
- The paper does not state the resolution, noise or FOV used in its own experiments beyond what the configs show.

## Q1. Which papers use HouseExpo for multi-robot / multi-agent tasks, and do any evaluate fused/merged map quality?

### Takeaway
Only two verified full-text works run multi-robot experiments on HouseExpo floor plans (SenseSwarm 2026 with 32-512 robots on 3 HouseExpo apartments; SPACE 2026 with up to 256 robots on 16 HouseExpo layouts); both share a single synchronized global grid with instantaneous propagation and report coverage/time (SenseSwarm also reports obstacle IoU 0.85 of the shared-map predictor at 0.1 m/cell) but no separate fused-map quality metric and no communication model. The widely cited multi-robot map-prediction papers (Spinos 2025, 4CNet 2024/25, Ma 2024, Explore-Bench 2022, NeuralCoMapping 2022, MAexp 2024) do NOT use HouseExpo. No paper was found that scores per-pixel accuracy of a fused multi-robot map on HouseExpo.

### Cited Findings
- SenseSwarm (Que et al., arXiv 2610.22905, 16 Oct 2026, Tsinghua/PKU/Peng Cheng Lab; no peer-reviewed venue listed): swarm sizes 32/64/128/256/512; environments = 3 HouseExpo apartments (HE-S/M/L, 2,439-9,574 m^2 free space) + 4 KTH corridor composites (3,752-14,870 m^2) + 5 HKUST BIM floors (10,376-24,015 m^2); grid 0.1 m/cell; predictor SenseMapNet (709 K params) run on tiled frontier windows of a shared occupancy grid; "updates are assumed to propagate instantly", no range/latency/loss model, no compression or bandwidth figures; metrics: time to 95 % sensed, time to 80 % "known" (~16 % faster on average than best baseline, up to 37 %), equal-time area lead +670 m^2 mean (up to +1,359 m^2), contact rate 0.52 per robot per 100 m at 512 robots; predictor obstacle IoU on a held-out swarm split 0.34 (single-robot original) -> 0.73 (obstacle-weighted BCE) -> 0.85 (BCE + soft clDice); leave-one-building-out on hkust_3f gain 10.8 % vs 14.4 %; 12 floors x 9 methods x 5 sizes x 3 seeds = 1,620 ARGoS simulations; no F1/SSIM and no separate merged-map metric — [arXiv html 2610.22905](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22905).
- SPACE (Que et al., arXiv 2606.29372, v2 16 Oct 2026, accepted IEEE ROBIO 2026): swarms up to 256; HouseExpo 16 home layouts + KTH 8 floors; "local field guidance on a synchronized shared map"; coverage time within ~2 % of greedy nearest-frontier; inter-robot contact rate reduced 4x-17x; no map-quality metric in abstract — [arXiv abs 2606.29372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29372).
- He, Feng, Jia, Liu, "Decentralized Exploration of a Structured Environment Based on Multi-agent Deep Reinforcement Learning" (ICPADS 2020, pp. 172-179, DOI 10.1109/ICPADS51040.2020.00032): cites HouseExpo (per S2 citation list); abstract: DME-DRL extracts structural patterns, works with and without communication, reduces travel distance ~10.84 % vs heuristics and reduces communication cost (unquantified); dataset not named in abstract; closed access — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/ICPADS51040.2020.00032); [S2 citation list](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:1903.09845/citations?fields=title,year,venue,externalIds,citationCount&limit=500).

- Lu, Zhao, Zhou, Mao, "Behavior Scheduling for Multi-Robot Path Planning in Unknown Environment With Communication Constraints" (IEEE T-ASE vol. 22, pp. 10417-10428, 2025, DOI 10.1109/TASE.2024.3523875): cites HouseExpo; potential-field behaviour scheduling keeps the team fully connected ~70 % of the planning process; TurtleBot3 experiments; dataset not named in abstract — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/TASE.2024.3523875).
- Spinos et al., "Map Prediction and Generative Entropy for Multi-Agent Exploration" (arXiv 2501.13189, Jan 2025, JHU-APL / DEVCOM ARL): NOT HouseExpo. Own 2-D procedural town generator; 2,000 train maps / 180,000 snapshots at 10 % coverage intervals, 2,000 test maps / 30,000 snapshots; maps 200 x 200 cells at 0.5 m/px (100 x 100 m) resized to 256 or 512; 3 robots, 10 m lidar; models LaMa / RePaint / Stable Diffusion v1.2 inpainting (SD chosen); LPIPS overall 0.095 / 0.120 / 0.088 (LaMa / RePaint / SD), FID 30.29 / 8.70 / 28.02; no IoU/F1/SSIM; simulated maps are "a perfect fusion of local maps" so fusion quality is not evaluated; prediction, fusion and frontier extraction run centrally; time to ~99.8 % accuracy threshold 147 s with Generative Entropy vs 238 s with Visible Entropy (62 % faster) — [arXiv html 2501.13189v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13189v1). (A web-search snippet claiming the authors "adapt HouseExpo" is contradicted by the full text — [ResearchGate snippet](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388354104_Map_Prediction_and_Generative_Entropy_for_Multi-Agent_Exploration).)
- 4CNet (Tan, Narasimhan, Nejat; arXiv 2402.17904 v3 Apr 2025): NOT HouseExpo. Diamond-Square heightmaps, 224 x 224 px = 15 x 15 m (6.7 cm/px), 75,000 map-trajectory pairs (20 % test) + 20,000 predicted-map pairs; 2-6 robots; robots exchange trajectories (not maps) within a 1.5 m lidar range under a "Communication Success Probability" (CSP) 25/50/100 %; map-prediction metrics at 100 % CSP: MSE 50.35, Obstacle-IoU 0.59, FSIM 0.55, Valid-Trajectory-Score 0.91 (vs AEM baseline OIOU 0.24, VTS 0.46; T-AEM 0.28/0.47; DB 0.29/0.15); exploration coverage with 3 Jackals in 15 x 15 m: 23/41/56 % (low/med/high energy) vs upper bound 27/54/71 %, in 30 x 30 m: 21/42/58 % vs 23/46/68 %; 108 trials; no merged-map metric — local PDF text `4cnet.txt` from [arXiv PDF 2402.17904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17904).
- Ma, Liang, Cao, Tan, Sartoretti, "Privileged Reinforcement and Communication Learning for Distributed, Bandwidth-limited Multi-robot Exploration" (arXiv 2407.20203, Jul 2024, NUS): NOT HouseExpo (4,000 training / 100 test "dungeon" maps of 100 x 100 m; nodes 4 m apart; sensor 20 m); 4 robots trained, 4 and 8 tested; learned message = 1 x 64 float32 = 256 bytes vs H x W int8 belief maps; up to 99.2 % less communication (5.7 MB vs 656.2 MB upload, 4 robots); travel-distance cost 2.4 % (abstract) — the comparisons section says "surpasses" by 2.4 %, an internal inconsistency; merged-map quality not evaluated — [arXiv html 2407.20203v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.20203v1).
- Explore-Bench (ICRA 2022, arXiv 2202.11931, Tsinghua): NOT HouseExpo — scenarios are Gazebo worlds converted to 2-D maps (10 hand-built) plus a procedural generator (rooms/corridors/loops/corners, "thousands"); 1 and 2 robots; metrics T_total (99 %), T_topo (90 %), sigma (per-robot area std) and overlap ratio r_o; multi-robot maps merged with `multirobot_map_merge` (Gazebo) or a shared grid array (grid sim); TurtleBot3 lidar 7 m; no merged-map accuracy metric — [ar5iv 2202.11931](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.11931).
- NeuralCoMapping (Ye et al., CVPR 2022, arXiv 2203.16319): Gibson and Matterport3D (not iGibson, not HouseExpo); trained on 9 scenes; >30 % step savings vs CoScan on MP3D scenes >300 m^2; aggregator-listed exploration rates ~99 % (unverified) — [arXiv 2203.16319](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16319); [PKU news](https://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/english/news/240649.htm).
- MAexp (ICRA 2024, arXiv 2404.12824): scenarios = random obstacles, mazes (Kruskal), indoor from Habitat scans, outdoor from STPLS3D; point-cloud representation; ~40x faster sampling; not HouseExpo — [arXiv html 2404.12824v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12824v1).
- Bayer & Faigl, "Decentralized multi-robot exploration under low-bandwidth communications" (Autonomous Robots 50(1), 2025, CC-BY): shares only robot positions (no maps); 5 simulated subterranean environments; 3 quadrupeds real; metrics time/distance/coverage; not HouseExpo — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1007/s10514-025-10234-3).
- PRoID (Kim, Baek, Corah, Best, Moon, Scherer; arXiv 2604.10433, Apr 2026): multi-robot exploration + relaying to a base station using learned map prediction; evaluated on "real-world indoor floor plan datasets" (names not in abstract); no numbers in abstract — [arXiv abs 2604.10433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10433).

### Inferences
- Both verified HouseExpo multi-robot papers (SenseSwarm, SPACE) come from the same Tsinghua group (Que/Gao/Qiao) and treat the shared map as a perfectly synchronised global grid, so they are measuring planning, not fusion; their "reconstruction (known-map) time" is the closest thing to a fused-map quality signal and is a coverage-type measure, not per-pixel accuracy.
- The ICRA 2023 Kim et al. paper is the only HouseExpo-citing work whose core contribution is multi-agent map fusion; given it cites HouseExpo it plausibly uses HouseExpo plans for its simulated rendezvous experiments, but this could not be verified (closed access).
- Consequently a per-pixel wall-F1 of a fused multi-agent map on HouseExpo (our setup's headline number) appears to have no published direct comparator; the nearest published numbers are single-robot completion scores (Q2/Q4) and SenseSwarm's shared-map obstacle IoU 0.85 at 0.1 m/cell.

### Gaps
- Full texts of Kim et al. ICRA 2023, He et al. ICPADS 2020 and Lu et al. T-ASE 2025 were not accessible (IEEE paywall), so their datasets, agent counts and any fusion metrics are unknown.
- Whether dPWM (ICRA 2026) or PRoID (2026) use HouseExpo could not be determined (no abstract / no dataset names).

## Q2. Best reported occupancy-map prediction/completion accuracies on HouseExpo and on KTH/other floor-plan sets, with observation regimes

### Takeaway
On HouseExpo the strongest verified per-pixel numbers are from Guzel & Uslu (ELCVIA 24(2):273-285, 2025): a residual U-Net on 128 x 128 whole-house grids reaches full-image IoU 0.796 / F1 0.884 / Acc 0.916 (ternary) and binary Acc 0.9235 / F1 0.9269 / IoU 0.8645, with inputs being sequential partial maps from a TurtleBot3 Voronoi-waypoint exploration in Gazebo (cells/metre not stated because each house is resized to 128 px). Zwecher et al. (ICRA 2021) report a minimum per-cell wall F1 of 0.92 on HouseExpo after threshold tuning (resolution not stated, 16 range sensors of 20-cell reach). On KTH the best wall F1 is 0.801 (P2 Explore FPUNet, 0.2 m/cell, 12 m lidar, local 128 x 128 window); SenseExpo reports SSIM 0.731 on KTH and only 0.605 cross-domain on HouseExpo (256 x 256 local windows). On Gibson/MP3D (RGB-D, 5 cm cells) OccAnt reaches per-frame IoU 56.5 % / F1 69.8 %.

### Inferences
- The only HouseExpo numbers computed the same way as ours (per-pixel, 128 x 128, house-fitted) are the ELCVIA 2025 ones: UNet binary F1 0.9269 / IoU 0.8645 and ternary F1 0.884 / IoU 0.796. Those targets are the complete map (not the union of observations), inputs are a single robot's sequential partial maps, and the metric is over the full image including the free/unknown classes, so they are not strictly comparable to our wall-only F1 0.89-0.90 (fused) / 0.92 (own decode); but the magnitude is similar.
- Zwecher's "minimum F1 0.92" is a wall-probability F1 after per-dataset threshold tuning and therefore closer to our "own-observation decode wall F1 0.92" in spirit; its resolution is unknown (sensor reach "20 cells" suggests a coarse grid).
- KTH-based wall F1 values (0.80 at 0.2 m/cell) are lower than HouseExpo values, consistent with KTH's larger, corridor-rich office floors and the harder regime of predicting far beyond the observed region (12 m lidar, 12.8 m window).
- Cross-domain evidence (SenseExpo SSIM 0.731 on KTH vs 0.605 on HouseExpo; MCN-SLAM HouseExpo-trained models dropping on real maps) shows HouseExpo-trained priors are quite domain-specific.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a HouseExpo prediction metric as a function of percentage explored; ELCVIA uses whatever coverage the Voronoi trajectory produced, MCN-SLAM uses a single first-iteration SLAM map.
- Shrestha 2019's and Katyal 2019's KTH/MIT numbers were not retrievable (IEEE-only); the SFU thesis PDF was not parsed.
- Guzel & Uslu SIVP 2025 ("Map Completion Using Generative Models on a Custom Dataset") abstract is unavailable in OpenAlex; unknown whether the custom dataset is HouseExpo-derived — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1007/s11760-025-04431-x).

## Q3. Are there HouseExpo-based benchmarks with sealed test splits, and what train/test protocol do papers use?

### Takeaway
There is no community leaderboard; the only "official" split is the repo's `map_id_train.txt` / `map_id_test.txt` (24,588 / 10,538 houses in the paper), and every later paper draws its own subset and split (5,000/200/100 at 64 px; 3,200/361/426 sequences at 128 px; 100 train / 4 test maps; 15,894 maps with 100 held-out evaluation maps; 3 or 16 apartments for swarms). Splits are by house/scene, and ELCVIA additionally keeps whole exploration sequences in one split to avoid temporal leakage; the ELCVIA dataset and manifests were promised "upon acceptance" but a released URL was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Repo ships `experiments/map_id_train.txt`, `map_id_test.txt`, `config_autonomous_exploration.yaml`, `config_obstacle_avoidance.yaml`, `empty.json/txt` — [GitHub experiments dir](https://github.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo/tree/master/experiments); the paper's exploration split is 24,588 train / 10,538 test — [ar5iv 1903.09845](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.09845).
- Repo also provides `pseudoslam/viz/map_id_set_generator.py --num 100` for sampling subsets — [GitHub README](https://github.com/TeaganLi/HouseExpo).
- MCN-SLAM: 5,000 / 200 / 100 (train/val/test) HouseExpo images at 64 x 64, plus 10 Gazebo test houses drawn from the test set — [arXiv html 2103.09071v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.09071v2).
- ELCVIA 2025: 3,200 / 361 / 426 at 128 x 128 with entire scene sequences in a single split; frames selected manually after automatic SSIM/UQI/L1 thresholds "proved unreliable"; "the customized HouseExpo dataset (processed splits and manifests) will be released publicly upon acceptance" — [ELCVIA PDF](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/elcvia/elcvia_a2025v24n2/elcvia_a2025v24n2p273.pdf).
- Li et al. RA-L 2021: 100 training maps, 4 held-out test maps (234 x 191, 231 x 200, 255 x 174, 235 x 174 px at 16 px/m) and one 531 x 201 scalability map; 100 trials per map — [ar5iv 2106.09257](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.09257).
- Zwecher 2021: 15,894 HouseExpo maps, 100 evaluation maps per dataset excluded from PPO training — [ar5iv 2109.08490](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.08490).
- GAN-SLAM: 35,126 maps, 1,000 held-out validation samples — [arXiv html 2504.19653](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19653).
- SenseSwarm: 3 HouseExpo apartments; predictor held-out split of swarm observations; leave-one-building-out test on an HKUST floor — [arXiv html 2610.22905](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22905). SPACE: 16 HouseExpo layouts + 8 KTH floors — [arXiv abs 2606.29372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29372).
- Azizi et al. ("HouseExpo++"): 8,729 plans after label filtering; 306,440 random-walk sequences; one walk per graph held out as "proxy" — [ar5iv 2012.04735](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.04735).
- KTH-side protocols for comparison: MapEx 80:20 by building with 10 test plans; P2 Explore 70/15/15 by scene (21 test); Ericson split by building — see Q2 citations.

### Inferences
- Because every paper re-rasterises and re-splits, cross-paper numbers on HouseExpo are not directly comparable; a report should state the split, resolution and target definition next to any number (as done here).
- The closest thing to a "sealed" HouseExpo benchmark for completion is the ELCVIA 2025 128 x 128 sequential dataset, if/when its manifests are published.

### Gaps
- A public download of the ELCVIA 2025 split was not located.
- Whether Fleet2D (IRC 2023) or Explore-Bench-style frameworks wrap HouseExpo with a fixed split was not verified.

## Q4. Does anyone report per-pixel wall/occupancy F1 or IoU of a reconstructed map on HouseExpo, and at what resolution?

### Takeaway
Yes, three works do: ELCVIA 2025 (128 x 128 whole-house grids; binary F1 0.9269 / IoU 0.8645, ternary IoU 0.796 / F1 0.884 for a U-Net; GEOM 0.779 / 0.874; RePaint 0.772 / 0.869), Zwecher et al. 2020/2021 (per-cell wall F1 >= 0.92, resolution unstated), and MCN-SLAM 2022 (64 x 64; F 0.4963 on HouseExpo, 0.6429 in Gazebo at T-5). Cells-per-metre is explicitly stated by none of them; ELCVIA's maps are saved at 0.01 m then resized to 128 px, so cell size scales with house extent.

### Cited Findings
- ELCVIA 2025: metrics "computed over FULL image"; discrete IoU/F1/Acc after snapping to {0, 0.5, 1}; U-Net uses 3-class logits with class-weighted CE; binary Acc 0.9235 / Prec 0.9261 / Rec 0.9280 / F1 0.9269 / IoU 0.8645; Gazebo worlds at 0.01 m, `map_saver` at 0.01 m, resized to 128 x 128; "even 1-2 px wall deviations can yield false frontiers or sealed passages" — [ELCVIA PDF](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/elcvia/elcvia_a2025v24n2/elcvia_a2025v24n2p273.pdf).
- Zwecher 2021: per-cell wall probability, "minimum F1 of 0.92" after threshold tuning; sensor reach 20 cells; grid dimensions h x w unspecified — [ar5iv 2109.08490](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.08490); Zwecher 2020 defines precision/recall/F1 on predicted cells and plots F1 vs number of observations — [ar5iv 2011.02043](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.02043).
- MCN-SLAM: accuracy / precision / recall / F-measure per pixel on 64 x 64 maps (tables in Q2) — [arXiv html 2103.09071v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.09071v2).
- SenseSwarm: obstacle IoU 0.85 of the shared-map predictor at 0.1 m/cell on a held-out swarm split (mixed HouseExpo/KTH/HKUST) — [arXiv html 2610.22905](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22905).
- Guzel et al. ISDFS 2024: IoU up to 0.8790 (dataset unnamed in abstract) — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/ISDFS60797.2024.10527317).
- Other HouseExpo prediction works report only perceptual metrics: SenseExpo (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS/FID), SenseMapNet (SSIM 0.78, LPIPS 0.68, FID 239.79), GAN-SLAM (FID, SSIM) — see Q2.
- Resolution conventions actually used on HouseExpo: 16 px/m (official exploration config; Li et al. RA-L 2021), 33 px/m (official obstacle config), 64 x 64 whole-house (MCN-SLAM), 128 x 128 whole-house (ELCVIA), 256 x 256 (GAN-SLAM generator input), 0.1 m/cell (SenseSwarm) — sources as above.

### Inferences
- For a HouseExpo house of 8-20 m extent, a 128 px house-fitted grid is 6-16 cm/cell, i.e. ELCVIA's protocol lands in the same cell-size band as our setup (this is a derived estimate; house-extent statistics were not found in text form).
- ELCVIA's numbers are the best published HouseExpo analogue to our fused wall F1 0.89-0.90, but differ in (i) single robot, (ii) target = complete ground-truth map rather than union of observations, (iii) full-image ternary/binary scoring rather than wall-only F1. Our wall-only F1 on a union-of-observations target is a strictly easier target than ground-truth completion, so the two should be reported side by side with that caveat rather than ranked.
- No HouseExpo paper reports F1/IoU of a map produced by merging several robots' partial maps; our fused-map wall F1 appears to be the first such number on HouseExpo.

### Gaps
- Exact metres-per-cell for ELCVIA, MCN-SLAM and Zwecher cannot be recovered from the texts.
- Whether ELCVIA's binary F1 counts walls only (occupied vs rest) or free vs non-free is not spelled out ("Binary results" follow the ternary table without definition).

## Q5. Does any work compress or quantise maps shared between robots on HouseExpo-like data?

### Takeaway
No HouseExpo-based work compresses or quantises shared maps: SenseSwarm/SPACE assume an instantly synchronised shared grid with no bandwidth model. Map-compression results exist only on other data: a CNN + Huffman + RLE codec for 2-D lidar OGMs (99 % reduction, 124.7 kb/s -> 720.4 bit/s, PSNR 50.6 dB, SSIM 0.975; Sensors 2024), learned 256-byte messages replacing map sharing (99.2 % less traffic at 2.4 % travel cost; Ma et al. 2024), task-driven map compression (Psomiadis et al. 2024), scan-selection map merging (Commerge 2026, up to 5,000x), and position-only sharing (Bayer & Faigl 2025).

### Cited Findings
- SenseSwarm: "updates are assumed to propagate instantly"; no compression/quantisation/bandwidth figures; only notes per-cell monotonic updates make incremental bandwidth "small" — [arXiv html 2610.22905](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22905).
- Zhang & Deng, "Deep Compressed Communication and Application in Multi-Robot 2D-Lidar SLAM: An Intelligent Huffman Algorithm" (Sensors 24(10):3154, 2024): 3-layer feature CNN (stride-2 layer reduces resolution to 1/4), Huffman -> hex -> run-length encoding, 6-layer recovery CNN; ~2,800 Gmapping maps for training (environments unnamed; not HouseExpo); compression up to 99 %; bandwidth 124.7 kb/s uncompressed vs 720.4 bit/s compressed (UWB link 12.5 kb/s); recovered map PSNR 50.649 dB, SSIM 0.975; two-robot hallway experiment; localisation error 3.59 / 3.43 cm, 0.72 deg; no IoU/accuracy — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11124910).
- Ma et al. 2024: learned 1 x 64 float32 messages (256 B) vs H x W int8 belief maps; 5.7 MB vs 656.2 MB upload (4 robots), up to 99.2 % reduction; dungeon maps, not HouseExpo; merged-map quality not evaluated — [arXiv html 2407.20203v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.20203v1).
- Psomiadis et al., "Multi-agent Task-Driven Exploration via Intelligent Map Compression and Sharing" (arXiv 2403.14780, 2024): sensors transmit compressed map data to a navigating robot, using a task-driven uncertainty measure from compression as reward (snippet-level only) — [arXiv abs 2403.14780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14780v2).
- Commerge (arXiv 2606.25386, 2026): LiDAR map merging by selecting a small set of keyframe scans; "reduces inter-robot communication by up to 5,000x while maintaining alignment accuracy" (snippet-level only) — [arXiv html 2606.25386](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25386).
- "Communication-Aware Map Compression for Online Path-Planning: A Rate-Distortion Approach" (arXiv 2506.20579) — title/snippet only — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20579).
- Bayer & Faigl, Autonomous Robots 2025: shares only robot positions; five subterranean simulation environments; reports a bandwidth-vs-time tradeoff — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1007/s10514-025-10234-3). (A web snippet attributing "~23.5 kB/s map-sharing bandwidth by compressing maps and sharing only changes" to a Springer paper could not be matched to this abstract and is unverified — [Springer link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-025-10234-3).)
- 4CNet avoids map sharing entirely by exchanging trajectories (GPS positions) within sensing range under a dropout probability (CSP 25/50/100 %) — local PDF text from [arXiv PDF 2402.17904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17904).

### Inferences
- Message-passing/fusion under a bandwidth budget on HouseExpo (our 8 communication rounds) has no published baseline; the nearest quantitative anchors are the Sensors 2024 codec (SSIM 0.975 reconstruction at ~1 % of raw bandwidth) and Ma et al.'s 256-byte messages, neither of which reports per-pixel wall F1.

### Gaps
- Full texts of Psomiadis 2024, Commerge 2026 and the rate-distortion paper were not read; their datasets and reconstruction metrics are unknown.

## Q7. Closest alternative floor-plan datasets and their typical reported numbers for completion/prediction

### Takeaway
KTH (165 campus plans; 140-164 after cleaning; 0.1-0.2 m/cell in use) is the standard comparison set for map prediction/exploration and carries the most published numbers (wall F1 0.80 at 0.2 m, SSIM 0.73 at 256 px, relative IoU gains); MIT campus (775 plans) is cited but rarely benchmarked; Gibson/Matterport3D 2-D projections at 5 cm/cell give per-frame IoU 56 %/F1 70 % (OccAnt) and exploration-map IoU 35-46 % (UPEN); CubiCasa5K, Structured3D, RPLAN and MSD are floor-plan parsing/generation sets with no occupancy-completion numbers found.

### Cited Findings
- KTH / MIT origin: Aydemir, Jensfelt, Folkesson, "What can we learn from 38,000 rooms? Reasoning about unexplored space in indoor environments" (IROS 2012, pp. 4675-4682): analysed 197 buildings, 940 floors, >38,000 rooms across MIT and KTH campuses and released the KTH dataset and tools (KTH-only counts not given in abstract) — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=What%20can%20we%20learn%20from%2038,000%20rooms%20Reasoning%20about%20unexplored%20space%20in%20indoor%20environments&per-page=2). HouseExpo paper quotes MIT = 775 and KTH = 165 floor plans — [ar5iv 1903.09845](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.09845). KTH plans are XML wall/door descriptions per room; 149 remain after de-duplication (MapEx) / 140 (P2 Explore) / 164 (Ericson) — [arXiv html 2409.15590v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15590v3); [arXiv html 2409.10878](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10878); [ar5iv 2406.09160](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.09160). MIT spatial model: >160 buildings, >800 floors, ~36,000 spaces (Whiting thesis) — [BU page](https://cs-people.bu.edu/whiting/resources/pubs/whitingMS_web.pdf).
- KTH numbers (resolution / regime / metric): P2 Explore wall F1 0.801 (0.2 m/cell, 12 m lidar, 128 x 128 local); SenseExpo SSIM 0.731 / PSNR 9.209 / FID 33.16 (256 x 256 local, m/cell unstated); MapEx relative IoU(occupied) AUC +22.9 % vs nearest frontier at 0.1 m/cell, 1,000 steps; Ericson information-gain error 7.26 % at ~0.124 m/cell, 4.5 m lidar; MapEx exploration +25.4 % vs nearest frontier / +12.4 % vs map-prediction baselines (average of metrics) — sources in Q2.
- Gibson/MP3D: OccAnt per-frame IoU 56.5 % / F1 69.8 % (5 cm cells, 101 x 101 local, RGB-D 128 x 128, depth to 3 m); UPEN exploration-map IoU 45.6 % noise-free / 25.8 % noisy at T = 500 (5 cm cells, 768 x 768 global, MP3D) — [ar5iv 2008.09285](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.09285); [ar5iv 2202.11907](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.11907).
- Synthetic/other: Katyal 2018 SSIM 0.899 -> 0.767 (5 x 5 m at 5 cm, 1.1x-2.0x extrapolation); Spinos 2025 LPIPS 0.088 / FID 28.02 (0.5 m/cell towns); 4CNet obstacle IoU 0.59 (6.7 cm/cell heightmaps); Luperto 10 Stage buildings, qualitative layouts — sources in Q1/Q2.
- CubiCasa5K: ~5,000 floor plans with >80 object categories; benchmark numbers are semantic segmentation (test mIoU ~57.5 % rooms, 55.7 % icons), not completion — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-20205-7_3); [EmergentMind summary (secondary)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/cubicasa5k-dataset). CubiGraph5K adds organisational graphs — [GitHub](https://github.com/luyueheng/CubiGraph5K).
- Structured3D: synthetic; used as density maps projected from 3-D for floor-plan vectorisation; no occupancy-completion numbers found — [arXiv 2211.15658](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.15658).
- RPLAN: Wu, Fu, Tang, Wang, Qi, Liu, "Data-driven interior plan generation for residential buildings", ACM TOG 38(6), 2019; ">80,000 annotated floor plans in raster format"; "predominantly simple, single-unit layouts with mostly rectangular rooms" (ResPlan 2025); HouseDiffusion evaluated on RPLAN — [rplanpy](https://github.com/unaisaralegui/rplanpy); [ResPlan](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14006v1); [ar5iv 2211.13287](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.13287). The exact figure 80,788 was not confirmed by any fetched source.
- MSD (Modified Swiss Dwellings; van Engelenburg et al., ECCV 2024): ">5.3K floor plans of medium- to large-scale building complexes, covering >18.9K apartments"; 4TU release holds 4,167 training plans with the test split withheld for an ICCV 2023 challenge; third-party listing 5,372 — [ECVA](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/html/7514_ECCV_2024_paper.php); [4TU record](https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/e1d89cb5-6872-48fc-be63-aadd687ee6f9); [GTS listing (secondary)](https://gts.ai/dataset-download/modified-swiss-dwellings-dataset/).
- ResPlan (2025): 17,000 vector-graph residential plans — [arXiv html 2508.14006v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14006v1).
- Zillow Indoor Dataset (CVPR 2021): annotated floor plans with 360-deg panoramas and 3-D room layouts — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/html/Cruz_Zillow_Indoor_Dataset_Annotated_Floor_Plans_With_360deg_Panoramas_and_CVPR_2021_paper.html).

### Inferences
- For a wall-F1-style comparison the only like-for-like external numbers are P2 Explore's KTH wall F1 0.801 (coarser 0.2 m cells, far extrapolation) and ELCVIA's HouseExpo F1 0.88-0.93 (similar cell size to ours). Our fused 0.89-0.90 sits between them and above every reported multi-robot prediction IoU (0.59 obstacle IoU in 4CNet, 0.85 in SenseSwarm), but all of those are prediction-into-unknown tasks whereas ours scores reconstruction of observed cells.
- Gibson/MP3D numbers (IoU 26-57 %) are much lower because they include RGB-D sensing noise, pose drift and anticipation beyond the field of view; they are not a fair comparator for lidar-on-floor-plan setups.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that runs occupancy-map completion/prediction on CubiCasa5K, Structured3D, RPLAN or MSD, so there are no "typical" completion numbers for those sets.
- KTH-only plan/room counts from Aydemir 2012 and the exact RPLAN count were not verified.
