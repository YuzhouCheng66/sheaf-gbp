# Learned occupancy-map prediction, completion and anticipation on indoor floor plans (2018–2026)

Scope note for the report writer: this file covers (1) map prediction for exploration, (2) occupancy anticipation in embodied AI, (3) generative floor-plan priors, (4) KTH / HouseExpo dataset works, (5) metrics. Every number is given with resolution and target definition where the source states them; values I derived arithmetically are flagged "(derived)". Items I could not verify are in the Gaps lists. Our comparison point (stated by the coordinator, not a literature item): HouseExpo house-fitted chart, 128x128 cells (6–16 cm/cell), decoding one agent's own 64-scalar code reconstructs its OBSERVED region at wall F1 0.92 (per pixel, exact) / 0.95 with 1-px tolerance; after fusing 8–16 agents the union of observed regions is reconstructed at wall F1 0.89–0.90. Our target is the observed region (union of agents' observations), NOT the unobserved remainder.

Access notes: arXiv PDFs could not be parsed by the fetch tool, so arXiv HTML / ar5iv renderings were used, plus local pdftotext on MapEx, OccAnt, UPEN, Ericson 2024 and 4CNet PDFs. The SFU repository hosting Shrestha's thesis was offline ("until late October"), NSF PAR refused connections (Saroya 2020), and Semantic Scholar rate-limited; those three works are therefore documented from DOI records / abstracts (OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar) and from how later papers describe them.

## Catalogue of surveyed works (year, venue, URL, dataset, resolution, input size, what is predicted)

### Takeaway
Roughly 35 works were located. The exploration-oriented line (Shrestha 2019 -> Katyal 2019/2021 -> Saroya 2020 -> SEER 2023 -> MapEx 2024/25 -> SenseExpo/MapExRL/P2-Explore 2025) mostly trains CNN/inpainting networks on KTH or HouseExpo at 0.05–0.2 m/cell with 100–256 px crops and, strikingly, most of them report NO per-pixel prediction-quality number at all (only downstream exploration gains). The embodied-AI line (OccAnt, UPEN, L2M, SEA) does report IoU/F1 but on Gibson/MP3D 3D-mesh projections, over local crops that include both visible and anticipated cells.

### Inferences
- The KTH dataset has been rasterised at three different resolutions in this literature (0.1 m MapEx/MapExRL, 0.2 m P2 Explore, ≈0.124 m Ericson), with different de-duplication (149 vs 140 vs 164 vs 184 plans). KTH numbers are therefore not cross-comparable even within the same dataset.
- HouseExpo likewise has no canonical benchmark split or resolution; each paper builds its own (64x64 whole-house, 256x256 frontier crops at 0.1 m, unspecified-resolution grids).

### Gaps
- Shrestha 2019 primary text (architecture, resolution, metrics) could not be obtained: SFU Summit repository was offline and the ICRA PDF is paywalled. Characterisations (VAE, 4 input channels, KTH-style real floor plans) come from citing papers.
- Katyal 2019 ICRA and Saroya 2020 IROS: only abstracts obtained; no numbers.
- Venues not verified for: SenseExpo, P2 Explore, arXiv 2412.12825, Spinos 2025, 4CNet (v3), FlatLands, SEA, Katsumata 2021, both Zwecher preprints, Rajasekar 2026.

## Key Question 1: Best reported quality of the predicted/completed map in the unobserved region AND in the observed region (HouseExpo, KTH, Gibson/MP3D, RPLAN/MSD)

### Takeaway
No surveyed work reports reconstruction quality of the OBSERVED region separately: inpainting-style methods hard-clamp or copy observed cells (so observed-region accuracy is trivially 1.0 and excluded), while embodied-AI methods score the whole local crop (visible + anticipated) together. The best "whole-crop" numbers are OccAnt(depth) occupied-class F1 0.75 / IoU 0.62 (Gibson, 101x101 cells at 2.5–5 cm) and L2M occupancy F1 0.65 (MP3D, 64x64 at 10 cm); the best "unobserved-only" numbers are FlatLands IoU 0.83 / F1 0.89 (binary floor, 3.9 cm, 256x256). On HouseExpo the best per-pixel figures are F1 0.636 / IoU 0.518 (occupied, 0.1 m, 80x80 frontier window) and whole-house 64x64 F-measure 0.50; the only HouseExpo "F1 ≥ 0.92" figure (Zwecher) lacks resolution and region definitions.

### Inferences
- Reported occupied-class F1 for learned completion of indoor maps clusters at 0.5–0.75 when the target includes substantial unobserved area (OccAnt 0.75 with ~3 m visible depth inside a 2.5–5 m window; L2M 0.65; HouseExpo frontier-window 0.64; Katsumata 0.50 from a single SLAM iteration). The unobserved-only IoU of 0.82–0.83 in FlatLands is for a binary floor mask at 3.9 cm, where thick "non-floor" regions dominate, so it is not a wall-level number.
- Our 0.92 exact / 0.95 1-px-tolerant wall F1 is on the OBSERVED region, i.e., it is an autoencoding/reconstruction figure, which none of the above report; the closest analogue is the 5-px-dilated whole-floor IoU of 0.83–0.85 reached by MapEx/MapExRL after a full exploration episode (mostly observed cells, 10 cm, KTH) — and even that needs a 50 cm tolerance to escape a 75–85 % plateau, which suggests exact-pixel wall agreement on thin KTH walls is hard (inference from their stated plateau).
- Zwecher's "F1 ≥ 0.92" is the only HouseExpo number in the same range as ours, but it is a full-map figure after many observations with unknown resolution and no observed/unobserved split, so it cannot be used as a comparable.

### Gaps
- No work found that separately reports accuracy on the observed region (verified absent in OccAnt, UPEN, L2M, MapEx, MapExRL, SenseExpo, Katsumata, 2412.12825, FlatLands; FlatLands explicitly excludes it by construction).
- No absolute predictor IoU for MapEx's LaMa ensemble (only relative AUC gains; absolute values only in Fig. 7 which the text does not tabulate).
- No HouseExpo work reports wall-only (as opposed to occupied-including-furniture) metrics; HouseExpo removes doors from the layout, so "occupied" in HouseExpo rasters is essentially walls, but no paper states this when reporting numbers.
- Shrestha 2019's own prediction-quality numbers unavailable (paper not accessible).

## Key Question 2: How "wall" vs "occupied" is handled (thin walls, doorways); any wall-specific F1 or tolerance-based (1-px) metrics?

### Takeaway
Most occupancy-prediction papers treat a single "occupied" class (walls + furniture + clutter) and evaluate per pixel; only the KTH vector-line works (Ericson's FloorGenT/Floorist, P2 Explore's denoiser) explicitly target walls. Tolerance-based evaluation exists but at coarse scales: MapExRL dilates both maps by 5 px (50 cm), floor-plan vectorisation uses a 10-px corner tolerance, and FlatLands uses a 7-px (~27 cm) boundary band. I found no work reporting a 1-px-tolerant wall F1.

### Cited Findings
- KTH source format: floor plans "described as XML files of wall and door locations for each room" — [arXiv 2409.15590](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15590v3); each room a polygon of segments "categorized as walls, doors or windows"; doors assumed open ("both transparent and passable"), windows "transparent but impassable", walls "non-transparent and impassable"; a heuristic inserts wall segments across doorways when door pairs are closer than 2 m total gap; target segments lying inside occupied cells are removed; occupancy grid has four labels (unknown, free, occupied, window); input grids are axis-aligned by a 90°-modulo LiDAR-angle histogram "which minimizes discretization error, which is important for recovering the visible line segments" — [arXiv 2406.09160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09160)
- Ericson 2024 evaluates walls not by pixel F1 but by predicted information gain error (cells), plus token accuracy 81.7 %; with window labels, predicted segment length outside the building perimeter drops 22.4 % (10.93 % -> 8.48 %) — [arXiv 2406.09160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09160)
- HouseExpo: structure stored as line segments in metres; "Doors are identified by subtracting a door cross-section from the ground cross-section, then removed from the layout"; rasterisation resolution is user-set — [ar5iv 1903.09845](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.09845)
- MapEx: observed map has three labels (free 0 / unknown 0.5 / occupied 1); metric = IoU of the occupied class within the building footprint — [arXiv 2409.15590](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15590v3); MapExRL adds 5-px (50 cm) dilation of both prediction and GT "as a tolerance buffer" and notes that without it IoU "plateaus at 75–85 % even with full coverage" — [arXiv 2503.01548](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01548)
- P2 Explore: a UNet++ "denoising" network maps the cluttered observed local map to a clean floor plan (walls only), then a UNet predicts the floor plan beyond; training adds random obstacles to KTH plans so that "floor plan" = structural walls — [arXiv 2409.10878](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10878v1)
- OccAnt: "A cell is considered to be occupied if there is an obstacle" (any obstacle from the 3D mesh; furniture included), with a second "explored" channel; GT includes occluded/out-of-view cells of the V x V window — [ar5iv 2008.09285](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.09285)
- Katyal 2019 abstract: "custom loss function that improves prediction accuracy at physical boundaries" (closest thing to a wall-aware loss in this line) — [Semantic Scholar record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/icra.2019.8793500?fields=title,year,venue,abstract,citationCount,authors); Katyal 2021 uses class-balanced CE with 5x weight on occupied and a 5x5 morphological closing — [ar5iv 2012.12142](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.12142)
- FlatLands: binary floor vs non-floor (no wall class); boundary analysis uses a band radius r = 7 px ≈ 27 cm at 25.6 px/m, conclusions hold for r in {5,…,9}; no distance-threshold IoU — [arXiv 2603.16016](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16016)
- 4CNet: "Obstacle Intersection over Union (OIOU) to measure only the ratio of overlapping obstacle pixels" (obstacle-specific IoU, no tolerance) — [arXiv 2402.17904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17904v3)
- Spinos 2025 notes that with sparse obstacles an empty prediction already scores ≈90 % accuracy, hence thresholds at 95 / 99 / 99.8 % — [arXiv 2501.13189](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13189v1)
- Floor-plan vectorisation/reconstruction metrics (tolerance-based, from the 3D-scan floor-plan literature): a corner counts as recovered if a GT corner lies within 10 px (Floor-SP, FloorNet; Raster2Seq adds angle < 5°); an edge counts only if both endpoints pass and the edge exists in GT; rooms matched at IoU > 0.5 or 0.7; RoomFormer corner F1 87.2 vs HEAT 82.5 on Structured3D — [Floor-SP arXiv 1908.06702](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.06702); [Raster2Seq arXiv 2602.09016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.09016); [RoomFormer CVPR 2023 supp](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/supplemental/Yue_Connecting_the_Dots_CVPR_2023_supplemental.pdf)
- Generative priors: House-GAN++ outputs per-room and per-door 64x64 masks; walls are not separate masks but arise from vectorisation (Floor-SP) where "adjacent rooms share wall segments, and doors are line segments on walls" — [ar5iv 2103.02574](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.02574); HouseDiffusion represents rooms and doors as polygon loops of corners, no explicit wall thickness — [ar5iv 2211.13287](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.13287); MSD's structure image contains load-bearing walls (thickness above the 60 % quantile) and columns; doors are stored (passage / door / front door) but not predicted — [arXiv 2407.10121](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10121v3)

### Inferences
- A "wall F1" in the sense of our comparison point (thin rasterised wall segments, doorways open) is closest in spirit to HouseExpo/KTH rasters, where occupied == structural wall because clutter is absent (HouseExpo removes doors; KTH stores walls/doors as lines). None of the HouseExpo prediction papers name it "wall F1", but their occupied-class F1 is effectively a wall F1.
- The MapExRL plateau (75–85 % IoU at full coverage without dilation) implies that even a "perfect" exploration on KTH rasters leaves ~15–25 % of occupied pixels mismatched at exact-pixel level; this is the strongest published hint that 1-px tolerance matters on thin walls, and it supports reporting both exact and 1-px-tolerant F1 as we do.
- Tolerance scales used in the literature (5 px = 50 cm; 7 px ≈ 27 cm; 10 px corners) are far looser than our 1 px (6–16 cm).

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports a 1-px (one-cell) tolerant wall F1 / IoU; no paper reports precision/recall of wall segments for completed occupancy grids (Ericson explicitly does NOT report segment precision/recall).
- Whether doorways are left open or closed in rasterised HouseExpo targets is not stated by any of the HouseExpo prediction papers surveyed.

## Key Question 4: Does any work compress the map into a small latent (tens to hundreds of numbers) and reconstruct it, reporting F1/IoU vs latent size?

### Takeaway
Only three works were found that explicitly encode an occupancy map into a small latent and decode it, and none does so on house-scale floor plans with F1/IoU vs latent size: D2M2N (toy 12x12–24x24 grids, 16–128-d embeddings, 90.1–99.9 % cell accuracy), Zacharia et al. 2025 (LiDAR range images -> 32–1024-d VAE latents, 256 chosen, metric reported only as bar charts), and Rajasekar & Ratnoo 2026 (512x512 maps -> 2048-d, no reconstruction metric). The floor-plan completion literature uses full-resolution image-to-image networks and never reports a latent-size sweep.

### Cited Findings
- D2M2N (Ishat-E-Rabban & Tokekar, arXiv 2310.07070, v2 Apr 2024): each robot keeps an H x 1 embedding of its occupancy belief (H ∈ {16, 32, 64, 128}, default 32) that can be decoded back to an X x Y belief map; percentage of correctly reconstructed cells (Table II, H = 16 / 32 / 64 / 128): 12x12 map 97.8 / 99.2 / 99.8 / 99.9; 16x16 96.9 / 98.8 / 99.6 / 99.9; 20x20 93.3 / 97.7 / 99.2 / 99.8; 24x24 90.1 / 95.8 / 98.4 / 99.4; "more than 90 %" for 24x24 -> 16-d (compression ratio 36); grid-world datasets (Tetris-shaped obstacles ~20 % occluded; floorplan-like ~35 % occupied), 300K–1.5M samples; neighbours exchange embeddings instead of maps; no IoU/F1, no bandwidth figures — [arXiv 2310.07070](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07070v2)
- Zacharia, Dharmadhikari, Alexis (NTNU), "Collaborative Exploration with a Marsupial Ground-Aerial Robot Team through Task-Driven Map Compression", arXiv 2509.07655 (Sep 2025): VAE (5 conv + FC) compresses voxel-aware LiDAR range images (16x1800 ground, 64x512 aerial; 0.2 m voxels) — not a 2D floor plan; latent sizes swept 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024; overall loss minimal at 256, occupancy-similarity (k-nearest-voxel) peaks at 256 (aerial) / 512 (ground); 256 chosen; compression ≈ 1:337 (ground) / 1:384 (aerial); keyframed latent traffic 0.173 kB/s vs 3375 kB/s raw point clouds; exact per-latent metric values only in a bar chart — [arXiv 2509.07655](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07655v1)
- Rajasekar & Ratnoo, "Latent Space Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Robot Exploration", arXiv 2601.01139 (Jan 2026): symmetric conv autoencoder for 1x512x512 grids, 7 Conv2D blocks to 128x4x4 = 2048-d latent (Tanh), mirrored decoder with sigmoid; 2,000 Perlin-noise maps (9:1 split); reconstruction shown qualitatively only (Fig. 5), no IoU/accuracy/F1, no latent-size analysis — [arXiv 2601.01139](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01139)
- Non-learned alternatives for bandwidth-limited map sharing: Larsson & Maity, "Communication-aware Hierarchical Map Compression of Time-Varying Environments for Mobile Robots", arXiv 2504.10751 (2025) — distortion vs description-size trade-off, no learned latent — [arXiv 2504.10751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10751v1); "A Sample-Based Approach for Hierarchical Information-Theoretic Compression of Probabilistic Occupancy Grids", arXiv 2609.27330 — [arXiv 2609.27330](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27330)
- VAE latents used for PREDICTION rather than compression: Xie & Dames "Stochastic Occupancy Grid Map Prediction in Dynamic Scenes" (VAE predicting future grids) — [arXiv 2210.08577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08577v2); Shrestha 2019 described as VAE-based by [arXiv 2412.12825](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.12825v1) (latent size unknown)
- Inpainting / completion models (LaMa, U-Net, diffusion) used by MapEx, FlatLands, Spinos, SenseExpo are full-resolution image-to-image networks; none reports a bottleneck size or a reconstruction-vs-latent curve — [arXiv 2409.15590](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15590v3); [arXiv 2603.16016](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16016); [arXiv 2501.13189](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13189v1); [arXiv 2503.16000](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16000)

### Inferences
- The D2M2N numbers are the only published "cell accuracy vs latent dimension" curve; scaled to our setting (128x128 = 16,384 cells from 64 numbers = compression 256:1, versus their 24x24 = 576 cells from 16 numbers = 36:1), our 0.92 exact wall F1 is a substantially harder regime than anything they test, and accuracy (dominated by free cells) is not comparable to wall F1.
- A fair literature baseline for "reconstruct observed region from a tiny code" would have to be built (e.g., a VAE on HouseExpo at 128x128 with a 64-d bottleneck); no published number exists to cite.

### Gaps
- No work found that compresses a house-scale 2D floor-plan occupancy map into tens–hundreds of scalars and reports wall/occupied F1 or IoU versus latent size; searches on "occupancy grid autoencoder latent reconstruction IoU", "VAE occupancy grid latent floor plan robot communication", and "learned map compression multi-robot latent" returned only the three works above.
- Zacharia 2025's per-latent-size metric values are in a figure only; not extractable.

## Comparability notes for our HouseExpo / 64-scalar / observed-region result

### Takeaway
Of all numbers in this file, only the whole-crop figures (OccAnt occupied F1 0.75 @ 2.5–5 cm; L2M occupancy F1 0.65 @ 10 cm; MapEx/MapExRL end-of-episode IoU 0.83–0.85 with 50 cm dilation) and D2M2N's latent-reconstruction accuracies share any ingredient with our setting (observed cells included, or a small code); none matches target (observed region only), class (thin walls, exact or 1-px) and resolution simultaneously.

### Cited Findings
- Target definitions actually used: OccAnt — full local V x V window including visible cells ([ar5iv 2008.09285](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.09285)); L2M/UPEN — full local crop / full global map vs ground-projected GT ([ar5iv 2106.15648](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.15648); [ar5iv 2202.11907](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.11907)); MapEx/MapExRL — whole building footprint, unknown cells replaced by prediction, observed cells kept ([arXiv 2503.01548](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01548)); FlatLands — unobserved valid region only, observed clamped ([arXiv 2603.16016](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16016)); Katsumata — whole 64x64 map vs full GT ([ar5iv 2103.09071](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.09071)); arXiv 2412.12825 — 80x80 window around a frontier (loss) with unspecified metric region ([arXiv 2412.12825](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.12825v1)); 4CNet — masked target region ([arXiv 2402.17904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17904v3)); Ericson — information-gain cells at frontier clusters ([arXiv 2406.09160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09160)).
- Multi-agent fusion analogues: Spinos 2025 fuses three robots' lidar maps before inpainting and evaluates with LPIPS/FID only ([arXiv 2501.13189](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13189v1)); 4CNet fuses neighbours' trajectories via a contrastive encoder and reports OIOU 0.51–0.59 ([arXiv 2402.17904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17904v3)); D2M2N fuses neighbours' 32-d embeddings through learned aggregators ([arXiv 2310.07070](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07070v2)). None reports reconstruction quality of the union of observed regions after fusion.

### Inferences
- The literature number most defensible to quote next to our 0.89–0.90 fused-union wall F1 is MapExRL's 0.83–0.85 end-of-episode IoU (KTH, 10 cm, occupied class, 50 cm dilation, whole floor), with the explicit caveat that (a) theirs is IoU not F1 (IoU 0.85 ≈ F1 0.92 if TP-dominated, derived), (b) it uses a 5-px tolerance versus our 1-px, and (c) their map is mostly observed by episode end but still contains predicted cells.
- Because every inpainting-style method clamps observed cells, our observed-region reconstruction task is structurally different: it measures how much of the observed structure survives a 64-scalar bottleneck, which is a compression question (Key Question 4), not a prediction question (Key Questions 1–2).

### Gaps
- No published single-agent or multi-agent work evaluates reconstruction of the observed-region union on HouseExpo; the comparison must be stated as "no direct literature comparable".
