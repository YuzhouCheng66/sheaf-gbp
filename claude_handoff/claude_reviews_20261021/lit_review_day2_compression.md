## 0. One-paragraph verdict

The k-bit result (6 bits/dimension free, 5 bits -3.7 pp, 4 bits -12 pp) is plausible and is what the information budget of a 64-scalar bottleneck predicts; it is not what the learned-compression literature would call a "quantization" result, because the literature's low-bit successes (1-3 bits per element in split computing, 6-30 bits per BEV pixel in V2X codebooks) all rest on thousands of redundant elements, whereas here each of the 64 scalars is a non-redundant index of a 2.5 m block. Two things weaken the claim as stated: the gate (1 pp) is below the project's own measured run-to-run noise (sd about 2.3 pp over four owned-DT reruns), and the whole curve is one seed on one synthetic world family. The byte accounting is as honest as the collaborative-perception norm (payload only, Where2comm/CodeFilling count fp32 or index bits and nothing else) and more honest than most because it charges the 64-bit presence mask, but it is not yet the "whole process" number the README demands, and against the project's own sealed strong-lossless baseline the new wire is roughly 2-4x cheaper, not 100x (100x only holds against the naive full-frame 2-bit raster). On restriction maps the report's conclusion (identity is right for a global-map objective; partial selection trades bytes for coverage/context) agrees with the cellular-sheaf literature; what the write-up undersells is that max-selection fusion is literally the harmonic flow of a lattice-valued cellular sheaf (Ghrist-Riess Tarski Laplacian; Riess-Ghrist asynchronous gossip theorem), which is exactly the result that licenses loops, asynchrony and idempotence - provided ties are broken by a global total order, which the current `select` does not do.

## 1. Question 1 - k-bit uniform quantization with STE versus the literature

### 1.1 What the project did (code facts)

- Encoder output `mu = tanh(.)`, rounded to `2^(MU_BITS-1)-1` levels per side with identity STE (`train_v348_kbit.py` L229-232); precision `q = 1e-4 + (4-1e-4) sigmoid(.)` (L148) is put on the wire with a `floor` log codebook of `2^Q_BITS-1` levels over [1e-8, 128] (L118-124), also identity STE (L128). Sources are quantized once; relays forward codebook values; the decoder reads the 127-level re-quantized posterior mean and `log(1/P)` (L334; code review N1). No rate term, no entropy model, fixed-rate k bits per symbol.
- Measured: 6 bits matches 8 (R8 .890 vs .885, fresh .892 vs .898), 5 bits -3.7 pp, 4 bits -12 pp (single seed 342101, 36 epochs). Empirical entropies of the 6-bit model on VALIDATION: H(mu)=5.44 of 5.98 possible bits, H(q)=4.57 bits over 29 of 63 used levels, H(mu,q)=9.07 of 12 bits.

### 1.2 Scalar quantization + STE vs additive-noise relaxation

- Ballé, Laparra & Simoncelli, *End-to-end Optimized Image Compression*, ICLR 2017 (arXiv 1611.01704) introduced additive uniform noise as the training surrogate for rounding, jointly with a differentiable rate term; Ballé, Minnen, Singh, Hwang & Johnston, *Variational image compression with a scale hyperprior*, ICLR 2018 (arXiv 1802.01436) and Minnen, Ballé & Toderici, *Joint Autoregressive and Hierarchical Priors for Learned Image Compression*, NeurIPS 2018 (arXiv 1809.02736) add hyperprior and context models. Theis, Shi, Cunningham & Huszár, *Lossy Image Compression with Compressive Autoencoders*, ICLR 2017 (arXiv 1703.00395) is the STE (identity-gradient rounding) line; Agustsson et al., *Soft-to-Hard Vector Quantization for End-to-End Learning Compressible Representations*, NeurIPS 2017 (arXiv 1704.00648) is the annealed-softmax line.
- Agustsson & Theis, *Universally Quantized Neural Compression*, NeurIPS 2020 (arXiv 2006.09952): the noise surrogate equals universal (dithered) quantization in distribution, which removes the train/test mismatch; their experiments show that test-time hard rounding of a noise-trained model is nevertheless *better* in PSNR than the dithered channel (the mismatch is not what hurts), and that annealed soft rounding improves further, "especially in models of lower complexity".
- Guo, Zhang, Feng & Chen, *Soft then Hard: Rethinking the Quantization in Neural Image Compression*, ICML 2021 (arXiv 2104.05168): training with STE (or annealing) "is equal to optimizing a deterministic autoencoder", learns a less expressive latent space, and in their Kodak ablations the STE-trained models are consistently the worst RD curves with unstable training at some rates; the remedy is noise-train, then fine-tune the decoder/entropy model with hard quantization (8.9% BD-rate over Cheng et al. 2020). Mentzer et al., *Finite Scalar Quantization: VQ-VAE Made Simple*, ICLR 2024 (arXiv 2309.15505) is the strongest STE-only counterexample: `round(floor(L/2) tanh(z))` with STE and no auxiliary loss works as a VQ replacement, but their own sweep finds levels per channel L_i < 5 "leads to subpar performance" and their recipe compensates with more channels (product codebook L^d).

What this means for v348: training each k from scratch with STE and no rate term is the Theis-2017/FSQ recipe, which is legitimate for a fixed-rate code; the literature's warning is specific to the low-bit end. Guo et al. and FSQ both say that below about 5 levels per dimension STE training itself degrades the encoder, independently of capacity. The project's 4-bit arm has 15 mean levels and 15 precision levels, so it is above FSQ's L >= 5 heuristic, but the b5/b4 collapse could still be partly an optimization artefact: nothing in the current design distinguishes "the information is not there at 5 bits" from "STE cannot find it at 5 bits". The cheap literature control is a b5 arm trained with additive-noise relaxation (noise of one quantizer step on `mu`) followed by STE fine-tuning (Guo 2021), or FSQ-style with the bound `(L/2) tanh` matched to k. If b5-noise recovers to within 1 pp of b8, the cliff is an STE artefact and 5 bits is in fact free.

### 1.3 Vector quantization and learned codebooks

- van den Oord, Vinyals & Kavukcuoglu, *Neural Discrete Representation Learning*, NeurIPS 2017 (VQ-VAE): STE plus commitment/codebook losses; known codebook under-utilisation. FSQ (above) shows that a bounded scalar grid matches VQ when the implicit codebook is large and uses about 100% of the codewords, whereas VQ "struggles with utilizing large codebooks".
- In collaborative perception the codebook line is: Hu et al., *Communication-Efficient Collaborative Perception via Information Filling with Codebook* (CodeFilling), CVPR 2024 (arXiv 2405.04966): task-driven codebook, integer indices on the wire, "applying code index representation reduces the communication cost by 208 times while maintaining the same detection performance", 1333x/1206x lower volume than Where2comm on DAIR-V2X/OPV2VH+; Shenkut & Vijaya Kumar, *Residual Vector Quantization for Communication-Efficient Multi-Agent Perception* (ReVQom), arXiv 2509.21464 (2025): 3-stage RVQ, 6-30 bits per BEV pixel, "at 18 bpp (455x) ReVQom matches or outperforms raw-feature CP", and the degradation with codebook size is graceful: DAIR-V2X AP@0.5 .666 (24 bpp), .651 (18), .609 (12), .558 (6).
- Relevance: the project's `mu` with 63 levels and near-uniform usage (H(mu)=5.44/5.98) behaves like a 1-D codebook index of the block configuration, not like a smooth quantity. Under max-selection the mean is never averaged on the wire, so the only place where the *ordering* of the 63 levels matters is the STE gradient and the decoder's CNN. A learned 1-D non-uniform codebook (soft-to-hard, or a per-coordinate learned grid as in FSQ's "per channel codebooks" related work) keeps the "64 Gaussian means + 64 precisions" constraint (the mean just takes values in a learned alphabet) and is the literature's standard move between 5 and 6 bits.

### 1.4 Bit-depth sweeps in feature compression and collaborative perception

- Cohen, Choi & Bajić, *Lightweight Compression of Intermediate Neural Network Features for Collaborative Intelligence*, IEEE OJCAS 2021 (arXiv 2105.07102; ICME 2020 version arXiv 2105.06002): post-training clipping + uniform quantization of split-DNN activations: ResNet-50 at 3 bits no loss, 2 bits < 1% Top-1 loss, 1 bit -4.9%; YOLOv3 "no loss ... all the way down to 16 levels (4 bits)", < 1% mAP at 2 bits; with entropy coding 0.6-0.8 bits per element at < 1% loss. Without clipping, 3-bit quantization of the same layer drops Top-1 from 75.8% to 59.7% - a cliff of the same shape as v348's, cured by a learned/model-based range.
- Wang et al., *V2VNet*, ECCV 2020 (arXiv 2008.07519): Ballé-2018 hyperprior codec trained with a rate-distortion objective after the main network is frozen; 417x compression (10.0 MB -> 0.02 MB per message) "marginally affects the performance"; LiDAR with Draco only 33x.
- Hu et al., *Where2comm*, NeurIPS 2022 (arXiv 2209.12836): no quantization at all (fp32 features, "we do not consider any extra data/feature/model compression"); the savings come from the spatial confidence map, i.e. from *not sending* most locations, which is the feature-space analogue of the owned "unobserved blocks are free" dividend.

Normalisation matters when comparing: those works have 10^4-10^6 elements per message and can lose bits per element because the task information is spread redundantly; v348 has exactly 64 elements that already sit at the README's information floor (F7: a 12-room layout needs about 290 bits, 20 rooms about 480 bits before the support outline; 64 x 6 = 384 bits for `mu` alone). The relevant literature analogue of "1-4 bits in V2V" is therefore not bits per coordinate but bits per message: 54 B here versus 1-16 KB per agent for CodeFilling/ReVQom (6 bpp x 128^2 BEV pixels = 12 KB). A fairer per-cell figure for the paper: 54 B x 8 / 16384 cells = 0.026 bit per map cell, 0.5 bit per *known* cell at 5.3% known.

### 1.5 Is the 6 -> 5 cliff plausible?

Yes, on three grounds, with one caveat.

1. Budget: the 6-bit `mu` channel is nearly saturated (H(mu)=5.44 of 5.98 bits, 63 of 63 levels used). Removing one bit removes about one sixth of a code that is already at the F7 information floor for the 20-room stratum; a code at capacity has no graceful path. ReVQom's codebook sweep (above) shows the same convexity at the low end (-1.5, -4.2, -5.1 pp AP@0.5 per step 24->18->12->6 bpp) on a far more redundant representation; Cohen et al. show that unclipped uniform quantizers collapse abruptly below 3-4 bits. A -3.7 pp step at 5 bits and -12 pp at 4 bits is within what these curves would predict for a code with no redundancy to spend.
2. Falsifiable corollary that can be checked from the existing run JSONs without GPU: if the cliff is a budget effect it must be stratum-dependent (rooms20 at k=5 should drop more than rooms6); if it is an STE artefact it should be roughly uniform across strata.
3. The report's wording "k-bit 线路在任意 k 下并集误差为 0" is correct for the *fusion* (selection forwards codebook values) but a reader will take it as "no quantization error"; say "zero composition error; the source quantization error remains and is what the b5/b4 arms measure".

Caveat (disagreement, see D2): the gate "K0 and R8 >= b8 - 1 pp" is below the project's own noise floor; owned-DT reruns gave R8 .848/.866/.882/.828 (sd about 2.3 pp), so "6 bits is free" is a single-seed statement at about 0.2 sd and "5 bits costs 3.7 pp" is about 1.6 sd. The conclusion is probably right (the b6 arm is also better than b8 on K0, R8, FPR and own@R8 simultaneously, which is unlikely under pure noise), but the paper needs the paired two-seed design already used for max-vs-sum before the sentence "6 bits is free" can carry a table.

### 1.6 What the literature suggests to go below 6 bits without losing accuracy (ordered by expected yield per GPU hour)

1. Decouple `mu` and `q` bits. The sweep ties them (k,k). H(q) = 4.57 bits over 29 used levels says a 5-bit (even 4-bit) log codebook for `q` is nearly free, while the cliff is probably entirely `mu`-driven. Arms (mu6,q4), (mu6,q5), (mu7,q4): zero new code paths, 11/10 bits per coordinate (46-50 B/message with the mask). This is the cheapest experiment in this review.
2. Rate term + entropy coding (Ballé 2017; Singh, Abu-El-Haija, Johnston, Ballé, Shrivastava & Toderici, *End-to-end learning of compressible features*, ICIP 2020, for the feature-compression case). Replace the fixed-rate alphabet by a learned factorized prior over (mu, q) symbols per block with a lambda sweep: the model then spends bits where blocks are complex and almost none on empty/uniform blocks, and the result is an RD curve rather than a staircase. Classical support: entropy-constrained scalar quantization with uniform thresholds is within 0.255 bit/sample of the rate-distortion bound at high rate for smooth sources (Gish & Pierce, *Asymptotically efficient quantizing*, IEEE Trans. IT 14(5), 1968), whereas fixed-rate quantizers cannot exploit peaked marginals. Given the near-uniform `mu` histogram today, the gain will come from training with the rate term (the encoder is currently rewarded for spreading mass), not from coding the present code (the measured 20%).
3. Conditional entropy model over neighbouring blocks (Minnen 2018 context model, Ballé 2018 hyperprior). The 8x8 code grid is spatially correlated (walls continue across blocks); a causal context over the 2-3 already-decoded neighbours in the same message is cheap for 30 symbols. Avoid a transmitted hyperprior: at 40-byte messages its side information would not amortize. Also entropy-code the 64-bit mask conditioned on public positions (blocks outside the 5 m sensing disc of every subtree member are certainly prior; the mask then costs a few bits, not 8 bytes).
4. Learned non-uniform `mu` codebook (soft-to-hard, Agustsson 2017; per-coordinate learned grid as in FSQ's related work) or learned step size / range (Esser et al., *Learned Step Size Quantization*, ICLR 2020, arXiv 1902.08153; Cohen et al.'s clipping result above) - the standard fix for a cliff between 5 and 6 bits.
5. Noise-train then hard-finetune (Guo 2021) or FSQ bounding for the b5 arm, as the control that decides whether the cliff is optimization or information.
6. Importance/presence maps are already in place (owned mask = Where2comm's spatial confidence map; demand-driven stalks of D3-5(b) = Where2comm's request maps); the remaining literature move is CodeFilling's "information filling" (do not resend blocks the receiver's subtree already covers at equal or higher precision), which under max-selection is the "silence when not the argmax" rule and costs no accuracy.

## 2. Question 2 - is the byte accounting fair, what is missing, and what baselines make a credible comparison

### 2.1 Fairness by the field's standards

Where2comm counts `log2(|M| x D x 32/8)` bytes of fp32 feature payload and explicitly excludes any compression; CodeFilling counts `log2(H W |M| log2(n_L) n_R / 8)` index bits; ReVQom counts `H W n_q log2 K` bits; V2VNet reports the actual entropy-coded bitstream. None charges headers, ids, timestamps or model distribution. By that standard `kbit_bytes_v348.py` (64-bit mask + 2k bits per non-prior coordinate, over-counting `log2(2^k-1)` as k) is at least as fair as the norm, and the sealed 172 B / 576-608 B packets were stricter (they include framing). So the per-message numbers are defensible *as payload numbers*; what is not yet defensible is calling them the project's cost, because README §3.7 and §8 define the cost as the whole process.

### 2.2 What is missing (and roughly how big it is at 54 B messages)

- Sender id and round/version: 4 bits for N <= 16; for the dynamic protocol the version vector is already charged separately (D3-7). Negligible, but must be listed.
- Link/transport framing: the field ignores it, the README does not. At 54 B payload a UDP/IP header (28 B) or an IEEE 802.15.4 MAC header (9-25 B) is 20-50% of the airtime; whatever radio is assumed must be stated once and applied to every baseline equally.
- Entropy-coder reality: 42.6 B is an ideal-entropy figure from VALIDATION marginals. A real coder needs a TRAIN-fitted model (cross-entropy on VAL, not entropy), termination/flush bytes (2-4 B per message for range/arithmetic coding, 5-10% here) and the assumption in `kbit_entropy_v348.py` that relayed coordinates follow the source-code marginal (under max-selection relayed winners are biased to high q, so this is conservative on q). Until it is implemented the number should be labelled "ideal" wherever it appears.
- Per-round growth: 30.5/64 non-prior coordinates is the converged subtree union; round-1 messages carry only own blocks (15% of 64 = 9.6 coordinates, about 22 B). Totals should be summed from the actual per-round masks, and idempotence allows silence once a message stops changing (at most diameter rounds), which the README's "最少发送次数" criterion rewards. The report's per-message 54 B is neither the first-round nor the total figure.
- Setup: forest construction from public positions (already recorded as `public_setup_bytes: 100` in the v327 ledger; `distributed_forest_construction_bytes: null`, `model_distribution_bytes: null`). Model distribution: 0.24 M encoder + 0.63 M decoder parameters at H128 (my count), i.e. about 1.7 MB in fp16 per agent, one-off; small per episode but it must appear in the table because the raw baselines need no model.
- Decoder-side cost (my count on this container, 2 CPU threads): decoder 0.81 GMAC and about 26 ms per decode at H128, 2.1 GMAC / 42 ms at H256; encoder 0.19 GMAC (64 blocks) at H128. Each agent decodes once per round it wants a map. A raw-map baseline merges by max over ternary rasters, essentially free. Report both.
- Quality is not matched: the method is at wall F1 about .89-.92 and acc .95-.96; the lossless baselines are at 1.0. README §8 says matched quality; either degrade the raw baseline (coarser resolution, lossy quadtree) to the same F1 or plot bytes against F1 and compare curves.

### 2.4 Baselines for occupancy grids that the literature would expect

Classical / lossless:
- Generic entropy coders on the ternary raster: zlib, Zstandard, LZ4, RLE, PNG. Dehler, Authaler, Thakur, Wodtko & Buchholz, *Analysis of Efficient Transmission Methods of Grid Maps for Intelligent Vehicles*, arXiv 2604.01753 (2026) compare exactly these for V2X grid-map transmission (patch-wise compression; Zstd best below about 800 Mbps, LZ4 above; quantization to 1 byte per mass reduces size 75% and "real-time V2X-based applications with lower bandwidths are only possible if the combination of compression and quantization is used"). The project already uses the best of full-zlib and delta-zlib; add PNG (2-D prediction) and a bi-level context-adaptive arithmetic coder on the two planes (known, wall), which is the standard for sparse bi-level images and is usually well ahead of Deflate on them.
- Raw sensor baseline: the local map is a deterministic function of 3 poses and 3 x 256 ranges (5 m range at 0.156 m/px is 32 px, about 6 bits per beam with a no-hit flag): about 576 B uncompressed per agent, less with delta coding across the three nearly coincident poses. This is the honest "raw" comparator because unknown cells cost nothing in it either.
- Cropped 2-bit raster: bounding box of the observed region (at most 64x64 px at 5 m range) plus a 4-byte header: about 1 KB per agent, i.e. 19x not 76x against 54 B.
- Vector/geometric lossless: Douglas-Peucker/Hough line segments of the first-hit lines. For the v293 generator (axis-aligned 0.3 m slabs, straight corridor) this is the dangerous baseline: the README's own F7 budget says the whole 12-20 room layout is 36-60 numbers, so an agent's partial view is tens of bytes at near-lossless quality. `mapping_multiroom_preparation_v293.json` already warns that such a codec must be charged for support/fragments/headers; it should nevertheless be run, because a 54 B learned code that loses to a 40 B polyline code on this data would not survive review, and on HouseExpo/furniture data the comparison flips.

Lossy / structured (bytes vs distortion):
- Quadtrees: Kraetzschmar, Gassull & Uhl, *Probabilistic quadtrees for variable-resolution mapping of large environments*, IFAC 2004; Nelson & Michael, *Information-theoretic occupancy grid compression for high-speed information-based exploration*, IROS 2015; Larsson, Maity & Tsiotras, *Q-tree search*, IEEE T-RO 36(6), 2020; Larsson & Maity, *Communication-aware Hierarchical Map Compression of Time-Varying Environments for Mobile Robots*, arXiv 2504.10751 (2025): leaf-count-constrained quadtree encoders of map innovations with explicit bandwidth constraints, evaluated on 128x128 probabilistic grids - the same size as H128; Psomiadis, Maity & Tsiotras, *Communication-aware map compression for online path-planning*, ICRA 2024. A quadtree at the leaf budget that gives wall F1 .89 is the natural matched-quality structured baseline.
- Octrees for 3-D (Hornung, Wurm, Bennewitz, Stachniss & Burgard, *OctoMap*, Autonomous Robots 2013) - cite as the storage standard, not as a 2-D baseline.
- Parametric maps built for communication: Corah, O'Meadhra, Goel & Michael, *Communication-efficient planning and mapping for multi-robot exploration in large environments*, IEEE RA-L 2019 (GMM maps exchanged instead of grids) - the "alternative compact representation" family; worth one sentence of related work.

Learned controls the field expects (collaborative-perception taxonomy: early/intermediate/late fusion):
- Late fusion: each agent sends its own 54 B code once; the receiver decodes each and takes the union of decoded maps. This isolates what in-network max-selection buys (a fixed-size message independent of the number of sources) against N codes per node.
- A single-agent learned codec with a rate term and no sheaf (Ballé-style on the ternary raster), fused late. This is the "no sheaf" learned baseline a compression reviewer will ask for.

Report, in addition to bytes per message: bits per delivered known cell, total bytes to reach a given F1 as a function of N and rounds (the README's F1-vs-cumulative-bytes curve), and the same for every baseline under the same tree and the same scheduling (convergecast + broadcast would let both the raw and the learned scheme send 2(N-1) messages instead of 22 x 8).

## 3. Question 3 - restriction maps and the cellular-sheaf literature

### 3.1 Agreement with the report

- Hansen & Ghrist, *Toward a spectral theory of cellular sheaves*, J. Appl. Comput. Topology 3 (2019), and *Opinion dynamics on discourse sheaves*, SIAM J. Appl. Math. 81(5) (2021): the constant sheaf has identity restriction maps and its global sections are the consensus states; the previous literature round (`lit_review_v342_energy.md` §2) already concluded that the owned construction is "constant sheaf R^64 + support-restricted vertex potentials", and D3-5 confirms it empirically. Nothing in this session contradicts that.
- Sheaf neural networks: Hansen & Gebhart, *Sheaf Neural Networks*, arXiv 2012.06333 (2020); Bodnar, Di Giovanni, Chamberlain, Liò & Bronstein, *Neural Sheaf Diffusion*, NeurIPS 2022 (arXiv 2202.04579); Barbero, Bodnar, Sáez de Ocáriz Borde, Bronstein, Veličković & Liò, *Sheaf Neural Networks with Connection Laplacians*, ICML 2022 TAG workshop (arXiv 2206.08702). The learned-sheaf literature is explicit about *when* a non-trivial sheaf helps: the harmonic space of a non-trivial sheaf contains non-constant signals, which is what one wants for heterophilic node classification and against oversmoothing; for a task whose target is the constant section (everyone wants the same global map) the trivial sheaf is the right geometry, and learned maps (NSD learns `F = Phi(x_u, x_v)` from node features) would only add the ability to *disagree*. That is consistent with D3-5 and with v340's learned gains collapsing to the floor. So: no, there is no known use of non-identity restriction maps that adds information to a consensus target; they add representational freedom, not information.
- "Heterogeneous local frames" is the one genuine case of informative non-identity maps: Barbero et al. compute orthogonal maps that align neighbouring tangent frames (Singer & Wu, *Vector diffusion maps and the connection Laplacian*, CPAM 2012); in robotics that is the relative-pose transform between private frames. It transforms rather than adds information, and - important for this project - a translation by a fraction of a block mixes neighbouring block coordinates, so a private-frame version of the method is *not* diagonal-closed and would break the 64-diagonal-precision architecture (energy report §2(b)). The common public frame is therefore a load-bearing assumption, not a convenience; the limitations section should say so.
- Partial selection is diagonal-closed and is Robinson's sensor-integration sheaf (Robinson, *Sheaves are the canonical data structure for sensor integration*, Information Fusion 36, 2017); the demand-driven stalks of D3-5(b) are its request-driven form, and the zero-shot losses (7-10 pp inside the ROI) are a decoder-context effect, exactly as the report says, not a sheaf effect.

### 3.2 What the report undersells (disagreement D8)

Max-selection is not "sum-product with a different reduction"; it is the harmonic flow of a *lattice-valued* cellular sheaf. Ghrist & Riess, *Cellular Sheaves of Lattices and the Tarski Laplacian*, Homology, Homotopy and Applications 24 (2022) (arXiv 2007.04099) define, for a sheaf of lattices with Galois-connection restriction maps, the Tarski Laplacian `(Lx)_v = meet over edges of F^{+}(meet over neighbours of F(x_w))` and prove `Fix(id ∧ L) = Γ(X;F)` (fixed points of the discrete heat flow are exactly the global sections), with finite-time convergence under a descending chain condition. Riess & Ghrist, *Diffusion of Information on Networked Lattices by Gossip*, IEEE CDC 2022 (arXiv 2204.00167) extend this to asynchronous gossip: for any finite lattice-valued network sheaf with join-preserving maps and any firing sequence satisfying liveness, the sections are precisely the time-independent states of `x[t+1] = x[t] ∧ L_t x[t]`, on arbitrary graphs. The dual (join) flow is stated explicitly.

The v347/v348 protocol is this object: per coordinate the stalk is the finite chain of codebook pairs ordered by precision, the node state is the join of own and incoming factors, the constant sheaf's sections are "every node holds the global per-coordinate maximum", and D3-0's test results (idempotent, commutative, associative, flooding on loops equals the centralized maximum, 16 rounds == 8 rounds) are instances of that theorem. The demand-driven stalks are the same construction with edge lattices equal to sub-products (join-preserving projections), and the version-vector CRDT of D3-2/D3-7 is the same flow on a product lattice. So the honest statement is not "the sheaf is just ownership + selection" but: the Gaussian (vector-space) sheaf is constant with identity maps; the fusion rule that works is the harmonic flow of a lattice-valued sheaf on the same graph, whose theory supplies exactly the loop-safety, asynchrony and idempotence guarantees the project needs and that the equality-sum GBP lacks (energy report T7b). That is a stronger, citable and correct sheaf-theoretic claim.

One correction is required for the theorem to apply verbatim (disagreement D7): `select` (`train_v348_kbit.py` L304-309) breaks precision ties by candidate order (own first, then directed-edge order), which differs from node to node; two sources with equal quantized precision but different `mu` on the same coordinate can therefore win at different nodes, and the resulting state is not a global section (the code review's N6 notes ties become frequent at k=4). A join needs a total order: compare `(p_hat, mu_hat)` lexicographically (both are codebook values, so this costs no bits and no side information), or include the source id. With that change max-selection is a lattice join and the Riess-Ghrist convergence result covers loops and asynchronous schedules without further proof.

### 3.3 How to write it

State three things: (i) in a common public frame the Gaussian sheaf is the constant sheaf with identity maps and ownership lives in the vertex potentials (correct, and the diagonal-closure theorem says nothing else is compatible with 64 diagonal precisions); (ii) the fusion that composes partial maps exactly is the harmonic flow of a lattice-valued sheaf (Ghrist-Riess), which is why it is loop-safe, idempotent and asynchronous; (iii) non-identity maps re-enter only when the task changes (local-map objective: Robinson's cover sheaf with projection maps; private frames: connection-type maps, which break diagonal closure). Framed that way the sheaf language is neither decoration nor overclaim.

## 4. Question 4 - the three things a strict reviewer will demand on the compression claims, in order

Then state the ratio against each (today: about 64-80x vs (a), 2-4x vs (c), unknown vs (e)). The README's 100x is only reachable against (a) and the paper should not lead with it.
2. Statistical and external validity of "6 bits is free": paired two-seed runs (the gate is below the measured noise), a decoupled (mu-bits, q-bits) sweep, one noise-relaxation/soft-to-hard control at 5 bits to separate optimization from information, and the same curve on a second world family (HouseExpo with furniture).
